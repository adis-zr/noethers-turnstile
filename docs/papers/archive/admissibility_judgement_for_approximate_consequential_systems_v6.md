## Abstract

Approximate consequential systems act on tractable approximations while downstream workflows treat their outputs as permission to act. The recurring failure is not approximation error. It is **mislicensing**: a score, readout, or certificate is promoted into action authority it does not support. We call this evidence overextension.

We give a mathematical characterization of when a domain admits a principled remedy. For a claim class κ, let Ωκ be its action-state space, P a finite permission chain, and λκ : Ωκ → P the ideal licensing map sending each state to the strongest permission that would be sound if the ideal facts were known. For each threshold p, define the failure set F_p = {ω : λκ(ω) < p}. The central condition is **action monotonicity**: worsening the state cannot make stronger permission sound, so each F_p is upward closed under a natural worsening preorder.

The **Admissible Compilability Representation Theorem** proves that three conditions are equivalent, relative to a fixed monotone certifier language C:

1. Each threshold failure set F_p is generated by a finite C-obstruction basis.
2. The ideal licensing map factors through a finite monotone C-observable quotient.
3. There exists a bounded, sharp, monotone C-compiler for κ.

The canonical quotient Q_e^min — the coarsest exact permission quotient of λκ — is the central object. In mathematically grounded domains (approximate inference, matrix multiplication), Q_e^min is *derived*: the certifier language is canonical and the quotient is forced by the mathematics. In policy-grounded domains, Q_e^min is *chosen* among consistent quotients, and the governance artifact that records that choice is the profile.

When Q_e^min is not known in closed form, the **induction loop** reaches it constructively given adequate corpus (Theorem 9.5), and the result is order-independent (Corollary 9.6). The over-authorization signal driving the loop is a sharpness detector, not a correlation detector: it induces the minimal sufficient taxonomy, not a list of correlated features (Proposition 15.3).

We demonstrate the framework on the Epic sepsis model. Under a weak profile, Epic compiles to limited rollout. Under a corrected profile derived from FDA and NHS regulatory text — reconstructed by matching compiler-processable obligations to published guidance — Epic compiles only to experiment. The reconstruction shows both the power of the framework and its honest boundary: the compiler cannot choose thresholds; it can only enforce them once chosen.

We provide a deployable implementation as an open-source library. The compiler, profile registry, gap taxonomy, and benchmark suite are immediately usable. Retrospective audit of 16 documented harmful AI deployments across five domains shows that a single four-gap cross-domain profile would have blocked all 16 under Tier 1.

## 1. Introduction

There has been a decisive shift in how decisions are made. Approximate systems — clinical scores, retrieval results, agent plans, fraud scores, experiment readouts — are no longer used merely to inform human judgment. They are treated as permission to act. A score adequate for flagging a patient becomes support for operational rollout. A retrieval result adequate for surfacing a document becomes grounding for a legal summary. An experiment readout becomes rollout authority.

The recurring failure is not that approximate systems are wrong. It is that approximate evidence is used for a stronger purpose than it was certified to support.

Call this **evidence overextension**. Evidence overextension occurs when evidence valid for one permission level is silently promoted into authorization for a stronger one.

Formal methods and security systems solved an analogous problem for exact systems long ago. Access-control calculi, proof-carrying code, proof-carrying authorization, capability systems, and information-flow control all ask whether an action has the proof, authority, or capability it requires. The certificates are exact: a proof either type-checks or it does not.

Approximate consequential systems need the same discipline, but the object being controlled is different. The output is not a proof of correctness. It is an approximation whose authority depends on evidence, scope, context, provenance, and expiry. The right question is not "does this proof check?" but "what permission does this evidence actually support?"

This paper gives the mathematical characterization of when that question has a principled answer, constructs the object that answers it, and implements it as a deployable compiler.

The central object is the **ideal licensing map**

    λκ : Ωκ → P

where Ωκ is the action-state space for a claim class κ and P is a finite chain of permission levels. The map sends each state to the strongest permission that would be sound if all ideal facts were known. The compiler does not have access to λκ directly — if it did, there would be no approximation problem. What it has access to is a certifier language C: a structured family of observable predicates that can witness whether specific failure modes are present.

The **Admissible Compilability Representation Theorem** (Section 4) proves that a domain admits a sharp compiler exactly when λκ factors through a finite monotone C-observable quotient. This is not a tautology. The non-obvious content is the converse: if a sharp compiler exists, the failure sets must have finite certifier-observable structure. The compiler cannot be arbitrarily designed; it must match the actual failure geometry of the domain.

This theorem explains a striking asymmetry. In mathematically grounded domains — approximate Bayesian inference, approximate matrix multiplication — the failure geometry is determined by the mathematics, not by governance choices. The certifier language is canonical: "the approximate posterior is within ε of the true posterior" is a mathematical statement, not a policy choice. The quotient Q_e^min is forced. In policy-grounded domains — clinical deployment, hiring decisions, marketplace policy — the failure geometry must be specified by human judgment, and the profile is the governance artifact that makes that specification machine-checkable. The compiler enforces the profile mechanically, but it cannot choose what the profile should say. That asymmetry — derived versus chosen quotients — is the structural truth behind why the framework produces exact results on mathematical systems and auditable-but-not-unique results on policy systems.

We present the framework in five stages. Section 2 defines approximate consequential systems. Section 3 develops the ideal licensing map and action monotonicity. Section 4 states and proves the representation theorem, with the canonical quotient as protagonist. Section 5 develops the derived/chosen asymmetry. Section 6 gives the constructive reachability results and the causal separation theorem. Section 7 works through the Epic sepsis example in depth, showing how compiler-processable profiles reconstruct FDA and NHS regulatory obligations and where the framework reaches its honest boundary. Section 8 describes the implementation. Section 9 discusses tameness regimes. Section 10 identifies open problems.

## 2. Approximate Consequential Systems

A system is **approximate consequential** when four conditions hold:

1. The ideal output is unavailable at decision time.
2. The system acts on a tractable approximation of that ideal output.
3. A downstream workflow treats the approximate output as permission, authority, or control-relevant evidence.
4. The validity of the output depends on context that can change.

All four conditions matter. The first two distinguish approximate systems from exact systems: Paxos is consequential but not approximate in the relevant sense. The third is what makes the framework necessary: a prediction or score becomes dangerous when another system treats it as a license to act. The fourth is what makes the framework nontrivial for deployed systems: populations drift, repositories update, policies expire, model versions change.

The class includes clinical alerts, legal retrieval and summarization, agentic software deployment, fraud holds, hiring or credit scores, experiment readouts, marketplace policy changes, probabilistic inference used for action, and autonomous system interventions. It is not "AI systems in general." A model used only for offline analysis may be approximate but not consequential. A deterministic write may be consequential but not approximate. The framework applies precisely when an approximate output enters an authorization path.

## 3. The Ideal Licensing Map and Action Monotonicity

Fix a claim class κ. The mathematical setup has three components: the permission chain, the action-state space, and the ideal licensing map.

**Definition 3.1 (Permission chain).** A *permission chain* is a finite total order (P, ≤) where lower elements are more restrictive and higher elements are stronger permissions. A representative chain:

    OOC ≤ EXP ≤ REF ≤ UNS ≤ ETA ≤ ESC ≤ ROL ≤ DIA ≤ REV ≤ AEX ≤ ALR ≤ AAA.

The concrete labels are domain-defined. The total order is structural: it is what gives the compiler its key properties. Because P is totally ordered, the compiler can ask a well-defined question — what is the greatest permission supported by this proof context? — and return a unique answer.

**Definition 3.2 (Action-state space).** The *action-state space* Ωκ for class κ contains all facts relevant to the soundness of action under that class: ideal semantic facts, tractable approximations, candidate identities, contexts, intended uses, evidence, authority, scope, and runtime conditions. The compiler need not observe all of Ωκ. The ideal semantic components are often unavailable. Ωκ is the semantic space in which the target licensing map is defined.

**Definition 3.3 (Ideal licensing map).** The *ideal licensing map* is

    λκ : Ωκ → P.

The value λκ(ω) is the strongest permission that would be sound at state ω if all ideal facts were known.

**Definition 3.4 (Threshold failure sets).** For each p ∈ P, define

    F_p = {ω ∈ Ωκ : λκ(ω) < p}.

The set F_p contains all states where permission p is unsound. Because P is totally ordered, the failure sets are nested: p ≤ q implies F_p ⊆ F_q. Stronger permissions fail in at least as many states as weaker ones.

**Definition 3.5 (Worsening preorder).** A *worsening preorder* on Ωκ is a preorder ⪯κ where ω ⪯κ ω' means ω' is no better than ω for action-licensing purposes. Worsening includes weaker evidence, broader scope, stale dependencies, degraded calibration, missing provenance, absent authority, or more severe runtime conditions.

**Axiom 3.6 (Action monotonicity).** The ideal licensing map is *action-monotone* when

    ω ⪯κ ω'  ⟹  λκ(ω') ≤ λκ(ω).

If the action state worsens, the strongest sound permission cannot increase.

This is the one non-trivial semantic assumption. It is natural for any domain where "more evidence, tighter scope, fresher data, higher authority" reliably justifies "stronger action." If a domain violates action monotonicity — if worsening the state can make stronger permission sound — then the compiler discipline is not the right abstraction.

**Lemma 3.7 (Failure sets are upward closed).** If λκ is action-monotone, then each F_p is upward closed under ⪯κ.

*Proof.* Suppose ω ∈ F_p and ω ⪯κ ω'. Then λκ(ω) < p. By action monotonicity, λκ(ω') ≤ λκ(ω) < p. Hence ω' ∈ F_p. ∎

This lemma is the pivot of everything that follows. Once failure sets are upward closed, they become amenable to finite generation: in any setting with a tame preorder structure, upward-closed sets can be finitely described by their minimal elements.

## 4. The Representation Theorem

### 4.1 Certifier Languages

The theorem is stated relative to a certifier language, because without this restriction the result becomes trivial: if arbitrary predicates can be introduced as atoms, every failure set can be named by fiat. The language must be structured, and its structure must reflect genuine observability.

**Definition 4.1 (Monotone certifier language).** A *monotone certifier language* C over κ consists of (H, Param, Level, Atom, Sem, Induce) where H is a finite set of obstruction schemas; for each h ∈ H, Param_h is a parameter space (possibly infinite); Atom contains instantiated obstruction atoms a = (h, θ, ℓ); Sem assigns each atom an upward-closed subset ⟦a⟧ ⊆ Ωκ, where ω ∈ ⟦a⟧ means the obstruction named by a is present at ω; and Induce maps each judgment shape e to a finite induced atom set I_e ⊂ Atom.

The language is *schema-finite* because H is finite and *instance-finite per judgment* because I_e is finite. It may have infinitely many atom instances across all judgments — a production system sees fresh claims, candidates, and contexts continuously — but each individual judgment induces only a finite cube of observable predicates.

**Definition 4.2 (Observation map).** For each judgment shape e, define the finite Boolean obstruction cube B_e = {0,1}^{I_e} with coordinatewise order. The observation map α_e : Ω_e → B_e sets α_e(ω)_a = 1 iff ω ∈ ⟦a⟧. Because each atom is upward closed, α_e is monotone: ω ⪯κ ω' implies α_e(ω) ⊑ α_e(ω').

### 4.2 The Canonical Quotient

Before stating the main theorem, we identify the central object.

**Definition 4.3 (Canonical quotient).** The *canonical minimal quotient* of λκ restricted to Ω_e is

    Q_e^min = im(λ_e) ⊆ P

with β_min = λ_e : Ω_e → Q_e^min and inclusion i : Q_e^min ↪ P.

**Proposition 4.4 (Coarseness).** Q_e^min is the coarsest exact quotient of λ_e. For any exact factorization Ω_e → Q → P, there is a unique map h : Q → Q_e^min such that β_min = h ∘ β.

The canonical quotient is the permission value itself: the coarsest object that knows exactly which permission each state deserves. Any sharp compiler must compute at least this much information. The representation theorem will say: a finite C-observable presentation of Q_e^min exists exactly when F_p has finite C-obstruction structure.

### 4.3 Main Theorem

**Theorem 4.5 (Admissible Compilability Representation Theorem).** Fix κ, P, Ωκ, an action-monotone λκ : Ωκ → P, and a monotone certifier language C. For each judgment shape e, let α_e : Ω_e → B_e be the observation map. The following are equivalent:

**(A) Finite obstruction basis.** For every judgment shape e and threshold p, there exists an upward subset U_{e,p} ⊆ B_e such that

    F_{e,p} = α_e^{-1}(U_{e,p}).

**(B) Finite monotone observable quotient.** For every e, the ideal licensing map factors as

    Ω_e --α_e--> B_e --q_e--> P

where q_e is antitone (v ⊑ w implies q_e(w) ≤ q_e(v)) and λκ|_{Ω_e} = q_e ∘ α_e.

**(C) Bounded sharp monotone compiler.** There exists a bounded observational, antitone, threshold-complete C-compiler for κ: a compiler K_e such that K_e(ω) = λκ(ω) for all ω ∈ Ω_e.

*Proof.*

**(A) ⟹ (B).** Given upward subsets U_{e,p}, define q_e(v) = max{p ∈ P : v ∉ U_{e,p}}. This maximum exists because P is finite and the bottom permission has vacuous failure threshold. For ω ∈ Ω_e and any threshold p, q_e(α_e(ω)) < p iff α_e(ω) ∈ U_{e,p} iff ω ∈ F_{e,p} iff λκ(ω) < p. Since this equivalence holds for all p in a finite chain, q_e(α_e(ω)) = λκ(ω). Antitonicity: if v ⊑ w and q_e(v) = r, every permission above r fails at v, and since each U_{e,p} is upward, it also fails at w, so q_e(w) ≤ r = q_e(v). ∎

**(B) ⟹ (C).** Set K_e(ω) = q_e(α_e(ω)). Bounded because α_e queries only I_e. Antitone by composition. Threshold-complete because K_e(ω) = q_e(α_e(ω)) = λκ(ω). ∎

**(C) ⟹ (A).** Given bounded sharp K_e = q_e ∘ α_e, set U_{e,p} = {v ∈ B_e : q_e(v) < p}. Antitonicity of q_e gives U_{e,p} upward closed. By threshold completeness, K_e(ω) < p iff λκ(ω) < p, so F_{e,p} = α_e^{-1}(U_{e,p}). ∎

**Corollary 4.6 (Positive obstruction formula).** Under condition (A), since B_e is a finite Boolean cube, each upward set U_{e,p} is the upset of a finite antichain of minimal elements. Therefore F_{e,p} is expressed by a positive formula over I_e:

    ∨_{m ∈ Min(U_{e,p})} ∧_{a : m_a = 1} a.

Negation is not primitive. Absence of obstruction must be certified by a positive witness, not by the failure to observe an atom.

### 4.4 What the Theorem Does and Does Not Say

The theorem proves that these three conditions are equivalent. It does not say every domain satisfies them. The constant-bottom compiler — which always emits the weakest permission — is sound for every domain and sharp for almost none. Soundness alone cannot characterize admissible compilability; sharpness is the right condition.

The theorem also separates three properties that are easy to conflate. **Boundedness** is the finite-query condition: the compiler observes finitely many atoms per judgment. **Monotonicity** is the structural condition: more obstruction cannot produce stronger permission. **Sharpness** is the representational condition: the compiler's denial regions match the ideal failure sets, not merely conservative over-approximations of them.

All three are needed. A compiler can be bounded and monotone without being sharp (it over-refuses). A compiler can be sharp and bounded without being properly monotone (its denial regions are not upward closed, so it has no coherent failure geometry). The theorem identifies the conjunction as the right object.

## 5. Derived Versus Chosen Quotients

The representation theorem identifies when a sharp compiler exists. It does not say whether the canonical quotient Q_e^min is unique — whether a domain's failure geometry forces a particular taxonomy — or whether multiple consistent quotients exist, leaving room for governance choice.

This distinction is the deepest structural feature of the framework.

### 5.1 Mathematical Domains: Derived Quotients

In mathematically grounded domains, the ideal licensing map is defined by the mathematics of the approximation, and the certifier language is canonical.

Consider approximate Bayesian inference. The ideal object is the true posterior p*(x | e). The approximate output is a tractable posterior q(x | e). The failure condition for action authority at level p is that the divergence between q and p* exceeds a threshold:

    F_p = {ω : D(q || p*) > ε_p}.

The certifier language has one canonical schema: `posterior_divergence_gap(q, p*, ε)`, meaning the gap between approximate and true posterior exceeds ε. The semantics of this atom is determined by the mathematics. The threshold ε_p may be fixed by the application, but the structure of the failure set is not a governance choice.

In this setting, Q_e^min is forced. Any two profiles that are both sharp for this class must represent the same failure sets — the same threshold divergence conditions. The taxonomy is not designed; it is discovered.

The same structure holds for matrix multiplication under certified approximation, for probabilistic programs under certified inference, and for formal verification under bounded model checking. In each case, the "gap" between approximate and ideal is mathematically defined, the certifier language is the language of that gap, and the canonical quotient is unique.

This is why the compiler produces exact results on mathematical systems. It is not evaluating a designed taxonomy. It is evaluating the canonical observable shadow of a mathematically defined failure geometry.

### 5.2 Policy Domains: Chosen Quotients

In policy-grounded domains, the ideal licensing map is not given by mathematics. It is the map that a domain expert would apply if they knew all relevant facts. The failure sets — the states where a given permission level is unsound — are not derived from a mathematical definition. They are specified by human judgment.

Multiple distinct profiles can be consistent with action monotonicity for the same domain. Whether clinical utility evidence should be required before operational rollout is not a mathematical question. A hospital can coherently require it or not require it. Both choices yield action-monotone profiles. Neither is derivable from the structure of the approximation.

In these domains, Q_e^min depends on which failure modes the governance process has chosen to recognize. A profile that requires `clinical_utility_gap` to be bounded has a different Q_e^min than one that does not — they produce different permission boundaries on the same evidence.

This is not a weakness of the framework. It is an honest characterization of the domain. The compiler does not tell a hospital what clinical utility threshold is correct. It tells the hospital: once you have written down your threshold in compiler-processable form, the compiler will enforce it mechanically, consistently, and auditably.

### 5.3 The Asymmetry and Its Consequences

The derived/chosen asymmetry has several practical consequences.

In mathematically grounded domains, there is a fact of the matter about whether a profile is correct: it is correct iff it matches the canonical quotient. Induction from examples is a *discovery* process. The gap taxonomy being recovered already exists; the induction loop finds it.

In policy-grounded domains, there is no corresponding fact of the matter. Induction from examples reveals the *implicit* policy embedded in historical decisions, which may or may not match the policy the organization intends. The induction loop here is a policy extraction tool, not a discovery tool.

A second consequence: in mathematical domains, two independently derived profiles for the same class will converge to the same taxonomy (given adequate corpus and correct mathematics). In policy domains, two profiles derived by different governance processes may legitimately differ.

A third consequence: the compiler's claim to authority is different in the two cases. In mathematical domains, the compiler is sharp by mathematical necessity, not by governance fiat. In policy domains, the compiler is sharp relative to the recorded profile — and the profile is a governance choice that must be defended on its own terms.

Acknowledging this asymmetry is not a retreat from rigor. It is what makes the framework honest.

## 6. Constructive Reachability and Causal Separation

The representation theorem gives an existence result: when Q_e^min is a finite C-observable quotient, a sharp compiler exists. But for policy-grounded domains, Q_e^min is not known in closed form. The induction loop provides a constructive route to it.

### 6.1 The Induction Loop

**Setup.** Fix a judgment shape e with k domain gap types g_1, ..., g_k. The induction loop begins with an empty profile (only structural requirements) and iterates:

1. For each case in the corpus, compile under the current profile.
2. If the compiler emits a permission p ≥ ALR but expert judgment is p' < ALR (over-authorization signal), induct the first OPEN domain gap not yet required by the profile.
3. Repeat until no case triggers the over-authorization signal.

**Definition 6.1 (Clean witness).** A *clean witness* for gap g_i at induction stage j is a case where g_i is OPEN and all j−1 already-induced gaps are BOUNDED. A clean witness isolates g_i as the unblocked failure mode.

**Theorem 6.2 (Constructive reachability).** If the corpus contains at least one clean witness for every g_i, the loop converges in exactly k steps to the minimal sufficient taxonomy, which equals Q_e^min.

*Proof sketch.* At each stage j, the over-authorization signal fires on case c only when some gap outside the current profile is OPEN in c. By hypothesis, each g_i has a clean witness, so each g_i is eventually induced. Once all k gaps are in the profile, every failure case is blocked by at least one required gap; the signal no longer fires. The converged profile is minimal by definition of clean witness: removing any gap would restore a clean-witness case that would trigger the signal. By Proposition 4.4, the converged profile's denial regions match Q_e^min. ∎

**Corollary 6.3 (Order independence).** Let corpus X contain at least one clean witness for every g_i. For any permutation X' of X, the loop converges to the same set S_k = {g_1, ..., g_k}.

The induction *sequence* — which gap is induced first — depends on case presentation order. The induction *set* is order-independent, determined by Q_e^min rather than by history.

Corpus-size bound. Let p_i be the frequency with which g_i appears OPEN in failure cases, and p̄ be the typical open probability among already-induced gaps. The expected number of cases for full discovery is

    E[n] = Σ_{i=1}^{k} 1 / (p_i × (1-p̄)^(i-1)).

For k = 6 and p_i = 0.4 uniformly, E[n] ≈ 77, and empirically the minimum corpus is approximately 1.8 × E[n]. The basis is practically small for deployment-class domains.

### 6.2 Causal Separation

The induction loop does not pick up correlated non-causal gaps. This is a structural property of the over-authorization signal, not a statistical accident.

**Proposition 6.4 (Over-authorization signal as sharpness detector).** Let g_s be a gap type that is statistically correlated with failure — open in 70% of failure cases — but not in any minimal obstruction basis for F_{e,p}. The induction loop never induces g_s.

*Proof.* Once the profile contains the minimal obstruction basis G* = {g_1, ..., g_k}, the compiler blocks every failure case: by definition of G*, every failure case has at least one gap in G* open, which the profile requires to be bounded. The over-authorization signal does not fire. With no signal, no gap — including g_s — can be induced. ∎

**Corollary 6.5.** Even if g_s is OPEN in every failure case (perfect correlation), it is not induced if it is not necessary for blocking any case. The adequacy condition — signal silence — is a structural test, not a statistical one.

This matters for the policy-grounded case. A machine learning approach that regresses failure labels onto gap statuses would rank g_s highly. The induction loop produces zero false positives because it asks a different question: can the over-authorization signal still fire? Once it cannot, the loop stops. Each induced gap has a *witness case* — a specific case that would trigger over-authorization if that gap were removed from the profile. That witness case is the causal certificate: it is evidence that the gap is structurally required, not merely correlated.

## 7. The Epic Example: Reconstructing Regulatory Policy

The Epic sepsis model is an unusually well-documented instance of evidence overextension. It is not a case of absent evidence. It is a case of evidence being used for a stronger permission than it supported — specifically, AUC evidence being promoted into operational rollout authority. We use it to show three things: how the compiler makes this failure precise, how compiler-processable profiles reconstruct independently published regulatory obligations, and where the framework reaches its honest boundary.

### 7.1 The Failure as a Permissions Problem

Epic's evidence package at deployment time included model specification, approximation quality (AUC 0.76–0.83), calibration data, blast-radius analysis, and freshness documentation. What it lacked:

- **Clinical utility at the deployed threshold**: AUC measures ranking across thresholds; it does not certify that the operating-point PPV or sensitivity is adequate for the intended action class (generating automatic alerts reviewed by nursing staff).
- **Distribution shift across deployment populations**: validation evidence from one institution does not automatically support deployment across dozens of hospitals with different geographies, demographics, and clinical practices.

Under the compiler framework, these two absences correspond to two open gaps: `clinical_utility_gap` and `distribution_shift_gap`. The question the framework asks is not "is Epic a good or bad model?" It is:

    What permission did Epic's evidence actually support?

Under a profile that requires only approximation quality, model specification, calibration, blast radius, and freshness:

    Γ ⊢ epic_sepsis_model : ALR until ε₁

The compiler authorizes limited rollout. This is not a compiler error. It is a profile error: the profile did not ask the questions that mattered.

Under a corrected profile that also requires clinical utility and distribution shift:

    Γ ⊬ epic_sepsis_model : ALR
    Γ ⊢ epic_sepsis_model : AEX until ε₂

The same evidence, the same model, the same data — only the policy has changed. The compiler exposes the policy boundary.

### 7.2 Reconstructing FDA and NHS Obligations

The corrected profile was not derived from the Epic case alone. It was derived by translating published regulatory guidance into compiler-processable form. This translation is the most direct demonstration of the framework's value: it turns prose obligations into mechanical enforcement objects.

**FDA Draft Guidance for AI-enabled device software functions** (Docket FDA-2024-D-4488) requires, for devices in the substantial-equivalence pathway: sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV with pre-specified acceptance criteria and confidence intervals; multi-site validation; and prospective performance data where available. Translating this into the certifier language:

- `clinical_utility_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.operating_point_validation.v1` with fields `{ppv, sensitivity, npv, specificity, confidence_intervals, acceptance_criteria_prespecified}`. A token asserting AUC alone fails the contract: AUC is not a required field.
- `distribution_shift_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.multi_site_validation.v1` with fields `{site_count, site_demographics, performance_delta_across_sites}`. A token from a single institution fails the scope check.

**NHS RCR Guidance for AI deployment in medical imaging** requires shadow-mode testing on the local population before go-live, pre-defined acceptance criteria before procurement, and an ongoing post-implementation evaluation plan. Translating:

- `shadow_mode_validation_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.shadow_mode.v1` with fields `{local_population_size, performance_on_local_cohort, pre_go_live_duration}`. A model deployed without shadow testing has this gap open.
- `post_market_monitoring_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.monitoring_plan.v1` with fields `{monitoring_frequency, alert_thresholds, rollback_criteria}`.

The V3 profile that includes all four of these gaps is derived entirely from published regulatory text. It was not fitted to the Epic case. The same profile, applied to the CHARTwatch study — where clinical utility validation was conducted on the local population before deployment — compiles to ALR because the required tokens can be constructed from the published evidence.

The profile translation is constructive: given regulatory prose, a domain expert can produce a compiler-processable profile. The compiler then enforces that profile mechanically. What was previously "we require adequate clinical validation" — a phrase that can be interpreted to approve or refuse the same case depending on who reads it — becomes a structured object with concrete field requirements, semantic checks, and scope rules.

### 7.3 The Boundary: Where the Compiler Leans on Priors

The reconstruction also shows where the compiler is not self-sufficient. Two kinds of boundary cases arise.

**Threshold choices are outside the compiler.** The compiler can enforce "PPV ≥ 0.20 at the deployed threshold." It cannot derive 0.20 as the correct floor. A domain expert can set 0.15 or 0.25, write a well-formed profile, and the compiler will enforce whichever choice was made. The compiler is Lipschitz in the profile (Lemma M from the structural paper): a profile that differs from the intended profile by k entries cannot produce an output more than k permission steps wrong. But the compiler cannot identify which profile is correct.

This is not a deficiency. A threshold choice requires clinical judgment about the downstream consequences of false positives and false negatives in a specific care context. No mechanical system should make that choice. The compiler's value is that once the choice is made and recorded, it is enforced consistently rather than case-by-case.

**The compiler is anchored to its certifier language.** The over-authorization signal in the Epic case revealed `clinical_utility_gap` and `distribution_shift_gap` as necessary. But it could only reveal gaps whose types were expressible in the certifier language. Two gap types — `individual_population_gap` (population-level calibration does not certify individual-level prediction) and `feedback_coupling_gap` (model deployment changes future training distributions through its own outputs) — were not in the initial taxonomy. They were induced retrospectively from other documented harmful deployments.

This is the honest limit of constructive reachability. Theorem 6.2 says the loop reaches Q_e^min given a corpus containing clean witnesses. But Q_e^min is relative to the current certifier language C. If C does not contain a schema for a gap type, that type cannot be induced, and the converged profile may be sharp relative to C while remaining unsound relative to the true ideal licensing map. The certifier language is not automatically complete.

In the Epic case, a profile containing all four V3 gaps — clinical utility, distribution shift, shadow mode, and monitoring plan — would have blocked the harmful deployment. That profile is derivable from FDA and NHS guidance. The gap between the language of published regulatory text and the language of a first-pass certifier is where the framework depends on domain expertise to expand the taxonomy.

**The causal separation theorem sets a bound on spurious induction but not on omission.** Proposition 6.4 guarantees that the loop never induces a gap that is merely correlated with failure but not structurally required. It does not guarantee that every structurally required gap is in the certifier language. The asymmetry is: the compiler has a rigorous non-inclusion guarantee (no spurious gaps) but only a contingent inclusion guarantee (required gaps, if they can be expressed).

This boundary — rigorous enforcement of what is stated, honest silence about what is not stated — is the right posture for a formal system acting in a domain where human judgment is not eliminable. The Epic case demonstrates both sides: the compiler can recover the regulatory intent once the gap types are named, and it cannot recover intent it has no language for.

## 8. Implementation

The judgment form is

    Γ ⊢ z : p until ε

where Γ is the proof context, z is the candidate artifact or action, p is the emitted permission, and ε is the expiry condition.

The proof context Γ contains: membership, claim, candidate, context, scope, induced gaps, gap taxonomy version, gap profile version, proof tokens, proof token provenance, detail-contract registry version, expiry, allowed use, disallowed use, derivation, authority, runtime context, and audit.

The compiler:

1. Checks membership; emits OOC if out of class.
2. Induces the claim and gap set under fixed taxonomy version.
3. Checks expiry; emits EXP if fired.
4. For each token: checks registry validity, detail-contract conformance, liveness, and scope. Advances gap statuses only through valid witnesses.
5. Records structural failures (provenance mismatch, allowed-use conflict, scope empty, derivation invalid, failed negative controls, runtime context failure).
6. Applies authority ceilings, escalation requirements, rollback controls.
7. Searches positive permissions strongest-to-weakest; returns first satisfiable.
8. Meets positive result with structural failures and control outcomes.
9. Emits judgment and records blocking reasons for every denied stronger permission.

The core enforcement discipline: no approximation may be used as stronger permission than its evidence, scope, provenance, expiry, authority, and policy profile jointly support.

**Structural guarantees.** The following follow from the total order on P and standard lattice observations and are recorded without detailed proof: invalid tokens do not close gaps; no provenance means no proof; expired evidence cannot remain live; runtime can only downgrade; composition cannot upgrade weak evidence; authority ceilings cannot be exceeded; profile, taxonomy, and contract version changes require a new compile; absent required gaps fail closed; the emitted permission is no stronger than the profile, evidence, provenance, authority, scope, and expiry jointly support. These properties are consequences of the finite total order and the monotonicity established in the representation theorem. They are not independent contributions.

**Non-promotion under composition.** For n ≥ 1 envelopes, composed permission is the meet of component permissions. A stale or downgraded component cannot be hidden by composition with a fresh one. Decomposition cannot upgrade a child above its parent. Runtime revalidation can only downgrade. These are all consequences of meet in a total order.

**Benchmarks.** The implementation was validated against three benchmark suites:

*Structural tests* (EC-003 and EC-004): 22,423 combined tests covering the algebra, composition, decomposition, runtime, anti-laundering, token reuse, provenance identity, rollback, profile well-formedness, adapter determinism, and adversarial domain suites. Zero falsifications.

*Medical prospective benchmark* (MED-001): real models against PhysioNet Challenge 2019 data. 11/11 oracle cases and 9/9 adversarial cases, zero violations. The V1 → V2 → V3 profile progression was produced by this benchmark: V1 authorizes the Epic failure pattern; V2 adds clinical_utility_gap and distribution_shift_gap; V3 adds shadow_mode_validation_gap and post_market_monitoring_gap. Each profile transition was forced by matching the profile against a failure case or external regulatory standard.

*Retrospective audit* (CASE-LIB-001): 16 real-world AI deployments with documented harmful outcomes across medical, criminal justice, employment, autonomous systems, and government benefits. Three pre-registered profile tiers. Tier 1 — a single four-gap cross-domain profile requiring approximation quality, model specification, blast radius, and authority — blocked all 16 cases. The profile was derived independently from the observation that these four gap types recur across domains; it was not fitted to the cases.

**Library.** The compiler, profile registry, gap taxonomy, detail-contract registry, and benchmark suite are available as an open-source library. The library is immediately deployable: a domain adopter supplies a profile and a certifier for their gap types; the compiler handles the rest.

## 9. Tameness Regimes

The representation theorem requires that threshold failure sets admit finite C-obstruction bases. When does this hold? The abstract answer is: when the domain has some tameness principle that guarantees finite observable descriptions of upward-closed sets under the worsening preorder.

Two representative routes:

**Well-quasi-ordered discrete domains.** A preorder (X, ⪯) is a well-quasi-order (WQO) when every infinite sequence contains an increasing pair. In a WQO, every upward-closed set has finitely many minimal elements (otherwise the minimal elements form an infinite antichain). If the worsening preorder on Ωκ is a WQO, every F_p has a finite minimal bad basis, and the certifier language need only contain principal-upset atoms {ω : m ⪯κ ω} for certifiable minimal bad states m. Dickson's lemma (finite products of WQOs are WQO), Higman's theorem (finite-alphabet words under subsequence are WQO), and Kruskal's tree theorem give the classical examples.

**Semialgebraic approximation domains.** If hidden ideal variables live in R^m, visible state variables in R^n, and the compatibility relation and permission failure conditions are semialgebraic, then the visible failure set F_p^vis = {v : ∃s. Compat(s,v) ∧ Fail_p(s,v)} is the projection of a semialgebraic set. By Tarski-Seidenberg, projections of semialgebraic sets are semialgebraic. A semialgebraic set has a finite formula using polynomial equalities and inequalities, which serves as the obstruction basis. Threshold conditions in deployment practice — PPV ≥ t, calibration error ≤ δ, freshness age ≤ T — are semialgebraic by construction, making this route natural for real-valued evidence systems.

The effective smallness question — whether the basis is computable, not merely existent — is answered for deployment-class domains by the witness-rate bound of Theorem 6.2: for k gap types with open probabilities p_i, the expected discovery corpus is polynomial in k and linear in 1/p_i. For k = 6 and p_i = 0.4, approximately 200–400 cases suffice at the 99th percentile. Doubly exponential quantifier elimination is not a practical obstruction because the basis is induced empirically, not computed symbolically.

Other tameness regimes — Noetherian failure loci with finitely generated ideals, o-minimal structures with finite cell decompositions, tropical/polyhedral settings with finite polyhedral shadows — follow the same meta-principle:

    monotone threshold failure
    + finite/tame observable presentation
    ⟹ finite obstruction basis
    ⟹ admissibility compiler.

## 10. Open Problems

The representation theorem is static: it identifies when the projection object Q_e^min exists. Three subsequent theorems remain open.

**Composition.** For issued envelopes, conservative composition should satisfy a lax monoidal law in the obstruction order: α(E₁ ⊗ E₂) ⊒ α(E₁) ∨ α(E₂), meaning composition cannot hide an obstruction present in a component. Exact composition — equality — is stronger and generally false when evidence components are complementary. The gap between lax and strong monoidal composition is the operational signature of evidence laundering: a fresh component can obscure a stale one unless composition is forced through meet rules. A composition theorem would characterize precisely when lax-but-not-strong is the right regime.

**Expiry dynamics.** Static expiry is a monotone obstruction atom and fits the representation theorem without change. A dynamic theorem would define runtime operators R_t : Envelope → Envelope satisfying R_0 = id, R_{s+t} = R_s ∘ R_t, R_t(E).permission ≤ E.permission — a killed semigroup in stochastic settings. This requires a model of time evolution for evidence, context, authority, and expiry rules, which is not implied by the static representation.

**Geometric families.** The WQO and semialgebraic corollaries are two instances of a broader pattern. The Noetherian, o-minimal, and tropical routes are expected to yield analogous results by their respective finite-structure theorems. A unified geometric framework for admissible compilability across tameness regimes would clarify when the meta-principle applies and what effective bounds it produces.

## 11. Related Work

The closest ancestors of the admissibility compiler are the access-control and authorization calculi of the 1990s and 2000s. Abadi, Burrows, Lampson, and Plotkin gave an access-control calculus for distributed systems; Necula introduced proof-carrying code; Appel and Felten introduced proof-carrying authentication; Myers gave JFlow for information-flow control; Schneider characterized enforceable security policies. These systems ask whether an exact artifact satisfies an exact property. The contribution here is the extension to approximate artifacts whose authority depends on evidence, scope, provenance, expiry, and policy profile — objects none of the exact frameworks can directly handle.

Model cards, datasheets, AI governance frameworks, clinical validation standards, and legal AI audits address adjacent documentation problems. They specify or describe evidence. But documentation alone does not prevent downstream promotion. The novelty here is the compiled object and admissibility discipline: permission-valued judgments, explicit proof contexts, ordered permission levels, gap profiles, exact evidence provenance, live scoped evidence, expiry, immutable envelopes, runtime non-upgrade, and compiler-processable policy. This is not "formal methods for AI" generally. It is an authorization discipline for approximate evidence whose novelty lies in the representation theorem characterizing when such a discipline can be sharp.

The WQO tameness corollary has antecedents in the theory of well-structured transition systems (Abdulla et al., Finkel and Schnoebelen), where upward-closed sets and minimal bad sequences provide finite representations for infinite-state reachability problems. The connection is structural: both exploit upward closure under a well-quasi-order to produce finite descriptions. The action-state space here is not a transition system but the parallel is genuine.

---

## 12. Conclusion

An approximate consequential system is admissibly compilable — admits a bounded, sharp, monotone compiler — exactly when its ideal licensing map factors through a finite monotone certifier-observable quotient. This is the canonical object Q_e^min, and it is what the compiler evaluates.

In mathematically grounded domains, Q_e^min is derived: the certifier language is canonical, the failure geometry is determined by the mathematics, and the induction loop discovers a unique taxonomy. In policy-grounded domains, Q_e^min is chosen: multiple consistent profiles exist, and the framework's value is not that it selects the correct one but that it makes the choice explicit, machine-checkable, and auditable.

The Epic example shows both faces. The failure was not hidden: the evidence was publicly available, the deployment widely documented, the harm measurable. What was missing was a mechanical boundary between evidence and permission. Under the framework, that boundary is expressed as a profile, enforced by a compiler, and — once reconstructed from FDA and NHS guidance — would have blocked the deployment.

The compiler does not make weak evidence strong. It gives weak evidence a weak license, strong evidence a stronger license, and unsupported escalation an auditable refusal.
