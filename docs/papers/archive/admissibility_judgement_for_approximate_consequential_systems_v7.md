## Abstract

Approximate consequential systems act on tractable approximations while downstream workflows treat their outputs as permission to act. The recurring failure is not approximation error. It is **mislicensing**: a score, readout, or certificate is promoted into action authority it does not support. We call this evidence overextension.

We give a mathematical characterization of when a domain admits a principled remedy. For a claim class κ, let Ωκ be its action-state space, P a finite permission chain, and λκ : Ωκ → P the ideal licensing map sending each state to the strongest permission that would be sound if the ideal facts were known. For each threshold p, define the failure set F_p = {ω : λκ(ω) < p}. The central condition is **action monotonicity**: worsening the state cannot make stronger permission sound, so each F_p is upward closed under a natural worsening preorder.

The **Admissible Compilability Representation Theorem** proves that three conditions are equivalent, relative to a fixed monotone certifier language C:

1. Each threshold failure set F_p is generated by a finite C-obstruction basis.
2. The ideal licensing map factors through a finite monotone C-observable quotient.
3. There exists a bounded, sharp, monotone C-compiler for κ.

The canonical quotient Q_e^min — the coarsest exact permission quotient of λκ — is the central object. The derived/chosen asymmetry is not a property of certifier languages but of λκ itself. A domain is *derived* when the approximation structure uniquely determines λκ — there is one action-monotone ideal licensing map consistent with the mathematics, and the induction loop discovers it. A domain is *chosen* when multiple action-monotone λκ's are consistent with the domain's constraints — the profile does not describe a pre-existing λκ but constitutes one by committing to specific failure boundaries. In approximate inference, λκ is forced by the divergence geometry; two independently designed certifier languages targeting the same λκ produce identical permission boundaries. In clinical AI deployment, λκ is a governance commitment; P_with_utility and P_without_utility define different failure sets, corresponding to different ideal licensing maps over the same action-state space.

When Q_e^min is not known in closed form, the **induction loop** reaches it constructively given adequate corpus (Theorem 9.5), and the result is order-independent (Corollary 9.6). The over-authorization signal driving the loop is a sharpness detector, not a correlation detector: it induces the minimal sufficient taxonomy, not a list of correlated features (Proposition 15.3).

We demonstrate the framework on the Epic sepsis model. Under a weak profile, Epic compiles to limited rollout. Under a corrected profile derived from FDA and NHS regulatory text — reconstructed by matching compiler-processable obligations to published guidance — Epic compiles only to experiment. The reconstruction shows both the power of the framework and its honest boundary: the compiler cannot choose thresholds; it can only enforce them once chosen.

We provide a deployable implementation as an open-source library. The compiler, profile registry, gap taxonomy, and benchmark suite are immediately usable. Retrospective audit of 16 documented harmful AI deployments across five domains shows that a single four-gap cross-domain profile would have blocked all 16 under Tier 1.

## 1. Introduction

There has been a decisive shift in how decisions are made. Approximate systems — clinical scores, retrieval results, agent plans, fraud scores, experiment readouts — are no longer used merely to inform human judgment. They are treated as permission to act. A score adequate for flagging a patient becomes support for operational rollout. A retrieval result adequate for surfacing a document becomes grounding for a legal summary. An experiment readout becomes rollout authority.

The recurring failure is not that approximate systems are wrong. It is that approximate evidence is used for a stronger purpose than it was certified to support.

Call this **evidence overextension**. Evidence overextension occurs when evidence valid for one permission level is silently promoted into authorization for a stronger one.

Formal methods and security systems solved an analogous problem for exact systems long ago. Access-control calculi, proof-carrying code, proof-carrying authorization, capability systems, and information-flow control all ask whether an action has the proof, authority, or capability it requires. The certificates are exact: a proof either type-checks or it does not.

Approximate consequential systems need the same discipline, but the object being controlled is different. The output is not a proof of correctness. It is an approximation whose authority depends on evidence, scope, context, provenance, and expiry. The right question is not "does this proof check?" but "what permission does this evidence actually support?"

This paper gives the mathematical characterization of when that question has a principled answer, constructs the object that answers it, and implements it as a deployable compiler.

The central object is the **ideal licensing map**

    λκ : Ωκ → P

where Ωκ is the action-state space for a claim class κ and P is a finite chain of permission levels. The map sends each state to the strongest permission that would be sound if all ideal facts were known. The compiler does not have access to λκ directly — if it did, there would be no approximation problem. What it has access to is a certifier language C: a structured family of observable predicates that can witness whether specific failure modes are present.

The **Admissible Compilability Representation Theorem** (Section 4) proves that a domain admits a sharp compiler exactly when λκ factors through a finite monotone C-observable quotient. This is not a tautology. The non-obvious content is the converse: if a sharp compiler exists, the failure sets must have finite certifier-observable structure. The compiler cannot be arbitrarily designed; it must match the actual failure geometry of the domain.

This theorem explains a striking asymmetry — but the asymmetry is not about certifier languages. It is about λκ itself. In mathematically grounded domains — approximate Bayesian inference, approximate matrix multiplication — the approximation structure uniquely determines λκ. There is one action-monotone ideal licensing map consistent with the mathematics, and the certifier language is the language of that map's failure geometry. The quotient Q_e^min is forced, and the induction loop discovers it. In policy-grounded domains — clinical deployment, hiring decisions, marketplace policy — multiple action-monotone λκ's are consistent with the domain's constraints. The profile does not describe a pre-existing λκ; it constitutes one by committing to specific failure boundaries. Two profiles that both satisfy action monotonicity may define genuinely different failure sets — not two ways of expressing the same object, but two different objects. The compiler enforces whichever λκ the profile has chosen, mechanically and auditably. That asymmetry — derived versus chosen λκ — is the structural truth behind why the framework produces exact results on mathematical systems and auditable-but-not-unique results on policy systems.

We present the framework in five stages. Section 2 defines approximate consequential systems. Section 3 develops the ideal licensing map and action monotonicity. Section 4 states and proves the representation theorem, with the canonical quotient as protagonist. Section 5 develops the derived/chosen asymmetry. Section 6 gives the constructive reachability results and the causal separation theorem. Section 7 works through the Epic sepsis example in depth, showing how compiler-processable profiles reconstruct FDA and NHS regulatory obligations and where the framework reaches its honest boundary. Section 8 describes the implementation. Section 9 discusses tameness regimes. Section 10 identifies open problems.

## 2. Approximate Consequential Systems

A system is **approximate consequential** when four conditions hold:

1. The ideal output is unavailable at decision time.
2. The system acts on a tractable approximation of that ideal output.
3. A downstream workflow treats the approximate output as permission, authority, or control-relevant evidence.
4. The validity of the output depends on context that can change.

All four conditions matter. The first two distinguish approximate systems from exact systems: Paxos is consequential but not approximate in the relevant sense. The third is what makes the framework necessary: a prediction or score becomes dangerous when another system treats it as a license to act. The fourth is what makes the framework nontrivial for deployed systems: populations drift, repositories update, policies expire, model versions change.

The class includes clinical alerts, legal retrieval and summarization, agentic software deployment, fraud holds, hiring or credit scores, experiment readouts, marketplace policy changes, probabilistic inference used for action, and autonomous system interventions. It is not "AI systems in general." A model used only for offline analysis may be approximate but not consequential. A deterministic write may be consequential but not approximate. The framework applies precisely when an approximate output enters an authorization path.

## 3. The Ideal Licensing Map and Action Monotonicity

Fix a claim class κ. The mathematical setup has three components: the permission chain, the action-state space, and the ideal licensing map.

**Definition 3.1 (Permission chain).** A *permission chain* is a finite total order (P, ≤) where lower elements are more restrictive and higher elements are stronger permissions. A representative chain:

    OOC ≤ EXP ≤ REF ≤ UNS ≤ ETA ≤ ESC ≤ ROL ≤ DIA ≤ REV ≤ AEX ≤ ALR ≤ AAA.

The concrete labels are domain-defined. The total order is structural: it is what gives the compiler its key properties. Because P is totally ordered, the compiler can ask a well-defined question — what is the greatest permission supported by this proof context? — and return a unique answer.

**Definition 3.2 (Action-state space).** The *action-state space* Ωκ for class κ contains all facts relevant to the soundness of action under that class: ideal semantic facts, tractable approximations, candidate identities, contexts, intended uses, evidence, authority, scope, and runtime conditions. The compiler need not observe all of Ωκ. The ideal semantic components are often unavailable. Ωκ is the semantic space in which the target licensing map is defined.

**Definition 3.3 (Ideal licensing map).** The *ideal licensing map* is

    λκ : Ωκ → P.

The value λκ(ω) is the strongest permission that would be sound at state ω if all ideal facts were known.

**Definition 3.4 (Threshold failure sets).** For each p ∈ P, define

    F_p = {ω ∈ Ωκ : λκ(ω) < p}.

The set F_p contains all states where permission p is unsound. Because P is totally ordered, the failure sets are nested: p ≤ q implies F_p ⊆ F_q. Stronger permissions fail in at least as many states as weaker ones.

**Definition 3.5 (Worsening preorder).** A *worsening preorder* on Ωκ is a preorder ⪯κ where ω ⪯κ ω' means ω' is no better than ω for action-licensing purposes. Worsening includes weaker evidence, broader scope, stale dependencies, degraded calibration, missing provenance, absent authority, or more severe runtime conditions.

**Axiom 3.6 (Action monotonicity).** The ideal licensing map is *action-monotone* when

    ω ⪯κ ω'  ⟹  λκ(ω') ≤ λκ(ω).

If the action state worsens, the strongest sound permission cannot increase.

This is the one non-trivial semantic assumption. It is natural for any domain where "more evidence, tighter scope, fresher data, higher authority" reliably justifies "stronger action." If a domain violates action monotonicity — if worsening the state can make stronger permission sound — then the compiler discipline is not the right abstraction.

**Lemma 3.7 (Failure sets are upward closed).** If λκ is action-monotone, then each F_p is upward closed under ⪯κ.

*Proof.* Suppose ω ∈ F_p and ω ⪯κ ω'. Then λκ(ω) < p. By action monotonicity, λκ(ω') ≤ λκ(ω) < p. Hence ω' ∈ F_p. ∎

This lemma is the pivot of everything that follows. Once failure sets are upward closed, they become amenable to finite generation: in any setting with a tame preorder structure, upward-closed sets can be finitely described by their minimal elements.

## 4. The Representation Theorem

### 4.1 Certifier Languages

The theorem is stated relative to a certifier language, because without this restriction the result becomes trivial: if arbitrary predicates can be introduced as atoms, every failure set can be named by fiat. The language must be structured, and its structure must reflect genuine observability.

**Definition 4.1 (Monotone certifier language).** A *monotone certifier language* C over κ consists of (H, Param, Level, Atom, Sem, Induce) where H is a finite set of obstruction schemas; for each h ∈ H, Param_h is a parameter space (possibly infinite); Atom contains instantiated obstruction atoms a = (h, θ, ℓ); Sem assigns each atom an upward-closed subset ⟦a⟧ ⊆ Ωκ, where ω ∈ ⟦a⟧ means the obstruction named by a is present at ω; and Induce maps each judgment shape e to a finite induced atom set I_e ⊂ Atom.

The language is *schema-finite* because H is finite and *instance-finite per judgment* because I_e is finite. It may have infinitely many atom instances across all judgments — a production system sees fresh claims, candidates, and contexts continuously — but each individual judgment induces only a finite cube of observable predicates.

**Definition 4.2 (Observation map).** For each judgment shape e, define the finite Boolean obstruction cube B_e = {0,1}^{I_e} with coordinatewise order. The observation map α_e : Ω_e → B_e sets α_e(ω)_a = 1 iff ω ∈ ⟦a⟧. Because each atom is upward closed, α_e is monotone: ω ⪯κ ω' implies α_e(ω) ⊑ α_e(ω').

### 4.2 The Canonical Quotient

Before stating the main theorem, we identify the central object.

**Definition 4.3 (Canonical quotient).** The *canonical minimal quotient* of λκ restricted to Ω_e is

    Q_e^min = im(λ_e) ⊆ P

with β_min = λ_e : Ω_e → Q_e^min and inclusion i : Q_e^min ↪ P.

**Proposition 4.4 (Coarseness).** Q_e^min is the coarsest exact quotient of λ_e. For any exact factorization Ω_e → Q → P, there is a unique map h : Q → Q_e^min such that β_min = h ∘ β.

The canonical quotient is the permission value itself: the coarsest object that knows exactly which permission each state deserves. Any sharp compiler must compute at least this much information. The representation theorem will say: a finite C-observable presentation of Q_e^min exists exactly when F_p has finite C-obstruction structure.

### 4.3 Main Theorem

**Theorem 4.5 (Admissible Compilability Representation Theorem).** Fix κ, P, Ωκ, an action-monotone λκ : Ωκ → P, and a monotone certifier language C. For each judgment shape e, let α_e : Ω_e → B_e be the observation map. The following are equivalent:

**(A) Finite obstruction basis.** For every judgment shape e and threshold p, there exists an upward subset U_{e,p} ⊆ B_e such that

    F_{e,p} = α_e^{-1}(U_{e,p}).

**(B) Finite monotone observable quotient.** For every e, the ideal licensing map factors as

    Ω_e --α_e--> B_e --q_e--> P

where q_e is antitone (v ⊑ w implies q_e(w) ≤ q_e(v)) and λκ|_{Ω_e} = q_e ∘ α_e.

**(C) Bounded sharp monotone compiler.** There exists a bounded observational, antitone, threshold-complete C-compiler for κ: a compiler K_e such that K_e(ω) = λκ(ω) for all ω ∈ Ω_e.

*Proof.*

**(A) ⟹ (B).** Given upward subsets U_{e,p}, define q_e(v) = max{p ∈ P : v ∉ U_{e,p}}. This maximum exists because P is finite and the bottom permission has vacuous failure threshold. For ω ∈ Ω_e and any threshold p, q_e(α_e(ω)) < p iff α_e(ω) ∈ U_{e,p} iff ω ∈ F_{e,p} iff λκ(ω) < p. Since this equivalence holds for all p in a finite chain, q_e(α_e(ω)) = λκ(ω). Antitonicity: if v ⊑ w and q_e(v) = r, every permission above r fails at v, and since each U_{e,p} is upward, it also fails at w, so q_e(w) ≤ r = q_e(v). ∎

**(B) ⟹ (C).** Set K_e(ω) = q_e(α_e(ω)). Bounded because α_e queries only I_e. Antitone by composition. Threshold-complete because K_e(ω) = q_e(α_e(ω)) = λκ(ω). ∎

**(C) ⟹ (A).** Given bounded sharp K_e = q_e ∘ α_e, set U_{e,p} = {v ∈ B_e : q_e(v) < p}. Antitonicity of q_e gives U_{e,p} upward closed. By threshold completeness, K_e(ω) < p iff λκ(ω) < p, so F_{e,p} = α_e^{-1}(U_{e,p}). ∎

**Corollary 4.6 (Positive obstruction formula).** Under condition (A), since B_e is a finite Boolean cube, each upward set U_{e,p} is the upset of a finite antichain of minimal elements. Therefore F_{e,p} is expressed by a positive formula over I_e:

    ∨_{m ∈ Min(U_{e,p})} ∧_{a : m_a = 1} a.

Negation is not primitive. Absence of obstruction must be certified by a positive witness, not by the failure to observe an atom.

### 4.4 What the Theorem Does and Does Not Say

The theorem proves that these three conditions are equivalent. It does not say every domain satisfies them. The constant-bottom compiler — which always emits the weakest permission — is sound for every domain and sharp for almost none. Soundness alone cannot characterize admissible compilability; sharpness is the right condition.

The theorem separates three properties that are easy to conflate. **Boundedness** is the finite-query condition: the compiler observes finitely many atoms per judgment. **Monotonicity** is the structural condition: more obstruction cannot produce stronger permission. **Sharpness** is the representational condition: the compiler's denial regions match the ideal failure sets, not merely conservative over-approximations of them.

All three are needed. A compiler can be bounded and monotone without being sharp (it over-refuses). A compiler can be sharp and bounded without being properly monotone (its denial regions are not upward closed, so it has no coherent failure geometry). The theorem identifies the conjunction as the right object.

**Remark 4.7 (Sound-but-not-sharp is invisible to the over-authorization signal).** A compiler that under-authorizes — one whose denial regions strictly contain the ideal failure sets — never fires the over-authorization signal. The signal fires when K_e(ω) ≥ p but λκ(ω) < p: the compiler permits what the ideal map forbids. A sound-but-not-sharp compiler satisfies K_e(ω) ≤ λκ(ω) everywhere, so the condition λκ(ω) < p implies K_e(ω) ≤ λκ(ω) < p, and the signal cannot fire. Consequently the induction loop of Section 6 is a fixed point on any sound-but-not-sharp language: it converges in zero steps and produces no refinements, leaving the under-authorization intact and undetectable. The loop is a descent procedure in the permission order — it eliminates over-authorizations — but it has no mechanism for detecting or correcting under-authorizations. This asymmetry is a structural feature of the framework, not a deficiency: a sound compiler that under-authorizes is not mislicensing; it is being conservative. The framework's guarantee is non-promotion, not maximality.

**Remark 4.8 (Profile coherence as a separate well-formedness condition).** A profile is *coherent* for a claim class κ if every gap type required at every permission level is satisfiable by tokens available to that class. Incoherence occurs when a profile requires atoms producible only by token types that the claim class's natural evidence does not supply. An incoherent profile does not cause the compiler to fail — it causes a permission level to become structurally unreachable. The compiler correctly falls below the unreachable level, but the profile designer intended otherwise. Profile coherence is therefore a well-formedness condition separate from action monotonicity: a profile can be action-monotone and still be incoherent. Verifying coherence requires matching gap requirements against the token types that the claim class's certifier language actually produces. This condition is enforced by the detail-contract registry: a gap requirement is coherent only if at least one registered token type can satisfy it for the relevant claim class.

## 5. Derived and Chosen Licensing Maps

The representation theorem is stated relative to a fixed ideal licensing map

[  
\lambda_\kappa : \Omega_\kappa \to P.  
]

It characterizes when that map can be computed by a bounded monotone compiler over a finite observable quotient. It does not, by itself, explain where (\lambda_\kappa) comes from.

This section formalizes the distinction between two cases.

In a **derived** domain, the domain structure determines the licensing map once the action thresholds are fixed. The profile records an already determined failure geometry.

In a **chosen** domain, the domain structure leaves more than one action-monotone licensing map admissible. The profile does not merely record a pre-existing map; it selects one. In such domains, the profile is part of the constitution of (\lambda_\kappa).

The distinction is not a property of certifier languages. It is a property of the target licensing semantics.

### 5.1 Monotonicity Alone Does Not Determine the Licensing Map

A first attempt would be to say that a domain is derived when there is exactly one action-monotone map (\Omega_\kappa \to P). This is too strong, and in fact false in every nontrivial setting.

**Lemma 5.1 (Raw monotonicity is non-identifying).**  
Suppose (\Omega_\kappa) is nonempty and (P) contains at least two permissions. Then there is more than one action-monotone map (\Omega_\kappa \to P).

_Proof._ For every (p \in P), the constant map (\lambda_p(\omega)=p) is action-monotone, because for all (\omega \preceq_\kappa \omega'),

[  
\lambda_p(\omega') = p \le p = \lambda_p(\omega).  
]

Since (P) has at least two elements, at least two such maps exist. ∎

Thus derivedness cannot mean uniqueness among all monotone maps. It must mean uniqueness among maps satisfying the domain's licensing constraints.

Those constraints are not compiler constraints. They are semantic constraints: error tolerances, action thresholds, authority boundaries, risk definitions, intended-use restrictions, and any other facts that define what it means for a permission level to be sound.

### 5.2 Threshold Systems

It is useful to separate the action-state order from the threshold boundaries.

Let (P) be a finite permission chain with bottom element (\bot).

**Definition 5.2 (Threshold system).**  
A _threshold system_ on ((\Omega_\kappa,\preceq_\kappa,P)) is a family

[  
A = (A_p)_{p \in P}  
]

where each (A_p \subseteq \Omega_\kappa) is the set of states adequate for permission (p), satisfying:

1. **Bottom adequacy:** (A_\bot = \Omega_\kappa).
    
2. **Nestedness:** if (p \le q), then (A_q \subseteq A_p).
    
3. **Downward closure:** if (\omega' \in A_p) and (\omega \preceq_\kappa \omega'), then (\omega \in A_p).
    

Equivalently, (A_p) is downward closed under worsening: if a worse state is adequate for (p), then any better state is also adequate for (p).

Every threshold system induces a licensing map

[  
\lambda_A(\omega) = \max {p \in P : \omega \in A_p}.  
]

The maximum exists because (P) is finite and (A_\bot=\Omega_\kappa).

**Lemma 5.3 (Threshold systems and monotone licensing maps).**  
Threshold systems are equivalent to action-monotone licensing maps.

More precisely:

1. If (A) is a threshold system, then (\lambda_A) is action-monotone.
    
2. If (\lambda : \Omega_\kappa \to P) is action-monotone, then the sets
    

[  
A_p^\lambda = {\omega \in \Omega_\kappa : \lambda(\omega) \ge p}  
]

form a threshold system.  
3. These constructions are inverse to each other.

_Proof._  
First let (A) be a threshold system. Suppose (\omega \preceq_\kappa \omega'). Let (p=\lambda_A(\omega')). Then (\omega' \in A_p). Since (A_p) is downward closed and (\omega) is no worse than (\omega'), (\omega \in A_p). Therefore (\lambda_A(\omega)\ge p=\lambda_A(\omega')), so

[  
\lambda_A(\omega') \le \lambda_A(\omega).  
]

Thus (\lambda_A) is action-monotone.

Conversely, let (\lambda) be action-monotone. Bottom adequacy holds because (\lambda(\omega)\ge \bot) for all (\omega). Nestedness follows because if (q\ge p) and (\lambda(\omega)\ge q), then (\lambda(\omega)\ge p). Downward closure follows because if (\omega' \in A_p^\lambda), then (\lambda(\omega')\ge p). If (\omega \preceq_\kappa \omega'), action monotonicity gives (\lambda(\omega)\ge \lambda(\omega')\ge p), so (\omega\in A_p^\lambda).

Finally, starting from (A), the induced map (\lambda_A) satisfies (\lambda_A(\omega)\ge p) iff (\omega\in A_p), by nestedness. Starting from (\lambda), the induced threshold system recovers the same cut sets. ∎

Thus the mathematical object that determines (\lambda_\kappa) is not merely the preorder. It is the nested family of adequacy regions.

The corresponding failure sets are

[  
F_p = \Omega_\kappa \setminus A_p.  
]

Each (F_p) is upward closed. The representation theorem asks whether these upward-closed failure sets admit finite certifier-observable obstruction bases.

### 5.3 Domain Licensing Specifications

We now define derivedness relative to the domain constraints that determine which threshold systems are admissible.

**Definition 5.4 (Domain licensing specification).**  
A _domain licensing specification_ for claim class (\kappa) is a tuple

[  
\mathfrak S_\kappa =  
(\Omega_\kappa,\preceq_\kappa,P,\mathcal T_\kappa)  
]

where (\mathcal T_\kappa) is a nonempty set of admissible threshold systems on ((\Omega_\kappa,\preceq_\kappa,P)).

The associated admissible licensing family is

# [  
\Lambda_\kappa(\mathfrak S_\kappa)

{\lambda_A : A \in \mathcal T_\kappa}.  
]

The elements of (\mathcal T_\kappa) are the threshold systems consistent with the domain's semantic constraints. Those constraints may come from mathematics, regulation, clinical policy, product policy, safety standards, institutional risk tolerance, or some combination of these. The compiler does not choose (\mathcal T_\kappa). It only enforces a chosen element once one has been fixed.

**Definition 5.5 (Derived and chosen domains).**  
A claim class (\kappa) is _derived_ relative to (\mathfrak S_\kappa) when

[  
|\Lambda_\kappa(\mathfrak S_\kappa)| = 1.  
]

It is _chosen_ relative to (\mathfrak S_\kappa) when

[  
|\Lambda_\kappa(\mathfrak S_\kappa)| > 1.  
]

A derived domain has a unique admissible ideal licensing map. A chosen domain has multiple admissible ideal licensing maps, and an additional governance act is required to select one.

This definition fixes the defect in the raw monotonicity formulation. Monotonicity is necessary, but not identifying. Derivedness is uniqueness after imposing the domain's licensing specification.

### 5.4 Profiles as Selections of Licensing Maps

A profile is not merely an implementation detail. It is a finite, compiler-processable presentation of threshold boundaries.

**Definition 5.6 (Profile selection).**  
A profile (\pi) for (\mathfrak S_\kappa) is a constraint on admissible threshold systems. Write

[  
\mathcal T_\kappa[\pi] \subseteq \mathcal T_\kappa  
]

for the threshold systems satisfying the profile. The profile is _licensing-complete_ when

[  
|{\lambda_A : A \in \mathcal T_\kappa[\pi]}| = 1.  
]

When (\pi) is licensing-complete, it determines a unique map, denoted

[  
\lambda_{\kappa,\pi}.  
]

In a derived domain, the base specification already determines (\lambda_\kappa). A profile may expose or implement that map, but it does not choose among alternatives.

In a chosen domain, the base specification leaves several maps admissible. A licensing-complete profile selects one of them. In that case, changing the profile changes the ideal licensing map. It is not a procedural update; it is a change in the target semantics.

### 5.5 Soundness and Sharpness Are Relative to the Target Map

The representation theorem is always relative to a fixed (\lambda_\kappa). The same certifier language and compiler can be sharp for one target map, conservative for another, and unsound for a third.

**Definition 5.7 (Sound and sharp relative to (\lambda)).**  
Let (K:\Omega_e\to P) be a compiler and (\lambda:\Omega_e\to P) an ideal licensing map.

(K) is _sound_ relative to (\lambda) when

[  
K(\omega) \le \lambda(\omega)  
\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega_e.  
]

It is _sharp_ relative to (\lambda) when

[  
K(\omega)=\lambda(\omega)  
\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega_e.  
]

**Proposition 5.8 (Soundness is target-relative).**  
Let (\lambda,\mu:\Omega_e\to P) be two licensing maps. A compiler that is sharp for (\lambda) need not be sound for (\mu).

_Proof._ If there exists (\omega) such that (\lambda(\omega)>\mu(\omega)), and (K) is sharp for (\lambda), then

[  
K(\omega)=\lambda(\omega)>\mu(\omega).  
]

So (K) over-authorizes relative to (\mu), hence is not sound for (\mu). ∎

This is why there is no target-independent notion of a canonical certifier language. A certifier language is adequate only relative to the licensing map it is meant to compute.

### 5.6 Derived Example: Divergence-Threshold Licensing

The cleanest derived case is approximate inference with a fixed divergence functional and fixed action thresholds.

Let (\Omega_\kappa) contain states of the form

[  
\omega = (p^*,q,\ldots)  
]

where (p^*) is the ideal posterior and (q) is the approximate posterior. Let

[  
d(\omega)=D(q\Vert p^*)  
]

be a divergence or error functional. Let (P) be a finite permission chain, and let

[  
\tau:P\to \mathbb R_{\ge 0}\cup{\infty}  
]

assign an error tolerance to each permission level, with

[  
p\le q \implies \tau(q)\le \tau(p).  
]

Stronger permissions require weakly smaller error.

Define the worsening preorder by

[  
\omega \preceq_\kappa \omega'  
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  
d(\omega)\le d(\omega').  
]

That is, (\omega') is no better than (\omega) when its divergence from the ideal posterior is at least as large.

For each permission (p), define

[  
A_p^{d,\tau}={\omega : d(\omega)\le \tau(p)}.  
]

**Theorem 5.9 (Uniqueness of divergence-threshold licensing).**  
For a fixed divergence functional (d) and fixed monotone tolerance profile (\tau), the threshold system

[  
A^{d,\tau}=(A_p^{d,\tau})_{p\in P}  
]

is the unique threshold system satisfying the divergence-threshold specification

[  
\omega \in A_p  
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  
d(\omega)\le \tau(p)  
]

for every (p\in P).

Consequently the unique licensing map satisfying that specification is

# [  
\lambda_{d,\tau}(\omega)

\max{p\in P : d(\omega)\le \tau(p)}.  
]

_Proof._  
First, (A^{d,\tau}) is a threshold system. Bottom adequacy holds when (\tau(\bot)=\infty), or more generally when the bottom permission has no positive evidence requirement. Nestedness follows from monotonicity of (\tau): if (p\le q), then (\tau(q)\le\tau(p)), so

[  
d(\omega)\le\tau(q)  
\implies  
d(\omega)\le\tau(p),  
]

and therefore (A_q^{d,\tau}\subseteq A_p^{d,\tau}).

Downward closure follows from the preorder. If (\omega'\in A_p^{d,\tau}) and (\omega\preceq_\kappa\omega'), then

[  
d(\omega)\le d(\omega')\le \tau(p),  
]

so (\omega\in A_p^{d,\tau}).

Now let (A) be any threshold system satisfying the divergence-threshold specification. For every (p),

[  
A_p = {\omega:d(\omega)\le\tau(p)}=A_p^{d,\tau}.  
]

Thus (A=A^{d,\tau}). By Lemma 5.3, this threshold system induces a unique action-monotone licensing map, namely

# [  
\lambda_{d,\tau}(\omega)

\max{p\in P:d(\omega)\le\tau(p)}.  
]

∎

This theorem is the formal sense in which approximate inference is derived. Once the divergence functional and action thresholds are fixed, the failure geometry is forced:

# [  
F_p^{d,\tau}

{\omega:d(\omega)>\tau(p)}.  
]

There is no governance choice about the shape of the failure set. A state either exceeds the divergence tolerance or it does not.

The numerical values of (\tau(p)) may still be application-specific. A clinical decision-support system and an offline exploratory analysis may choose different tolerances. But after those tolerances are fixed, the licensing map is determined by the approximation geometry. The profile records a threshold map already forced by (d) and (\tau).

This is the distinction between **value choice** and **structural choice**. Divergence-threshold domains may require a choice of numerical tolerances, but they do not require a choice about the form of the failure predicate. The form is derived.

### 5.7 Chosen Example: Clinical Utility at Rollout

Clinical deployment has a different structure. Even after the model, evidence, and action-state space are fixed, there may be more than one coherent answer to the question:

> What evidence is required for operational rollout?

Let (p=\mathrm{ALR}). Let (F_p^0) be the base ALR failure set induced by requirements such as model specification, approximation quality, calibration, blast radius, scope, freshness, and authority. Let (U) be the upward-closed set of states where deployed-threshold clinical utility evidence is absent or inadequate:

[  
U={\omega:\texttt{clinical_utility_gap is OPEN at }\omega}.  
]

Consider two ALR threshold systems:

# [  
F_{p}^{\mathrm{without}}

F_p^0,  
]

and

# [  
F_{p}^{\mathrm{with}}

F_p^0\cup U.  
]

Equivalently,

# [  
A_p^{\mathrm{without}}

\Omega_\kappa\setminus F_p^0,  
]

while

# [  
A_p^{\mathrm{with}}

\Omega_\kappa\setminus(F_p^0\cup U).  
]

The first profile does not require deployed-threshold utility evidence at ALR. The second does.

**Proposition 5.10 (Clinical utility as a genuine choice).**  
Suppose

[  
U\setminus F_p^0 \neq \varnothing.  
]

Then the two profiles above induce distinct action-monotone licensing maps.

_Proof._  
Since (F_p^0) and (U) are upward closed, both (F_p^{\mathrm{without}}) and (F_p^{\mathrm{with}}) are upward closed. Therefore their complements are downward closed adequacy regions, and each extends to a valid threshold system provided the surrounding permission levels remain nested.

Because (U\setminus F_p^0\neq\varnothing), choose

[  
\omega\in U\setminus F_p^0.  
]

Then (\omega\notin F_p^{\mathrm{without}}), so (\omega\in A_p^{\mathrm{without}}). But (\omega\in U), hence (\omega\in F_p^{\mathrm{with}}), so (\omega\notin A_p^{\mathrm{with}}).

Thus the two threshold systems disagree at permission (p), so their induced licensing maps differ. ∎

The disagreement region is exactly

[  
U\setminus F_p^0.  
]

These are states where all other rollout requirements are satisfied, but deployed-threshold clinical utility evidence is missing. One profile permits ALR. The other refuses ALR. Both are monotone. Both may be internally coherent. The difference is not syntactic. They are different licensing maps.

This is the formal sense in which clinical deployment is chosen at ALR. The profile is not merely a way to implement (\lambda_\kappa). It selects which (\lambda_\kappa) the institution is committed to.

### 5.8 Threshold-Local Derivedness

Derivedness is local to permission thresholds. A domain may be derived at one permission level and chosen at another.

**Definition 5.11 (Adequacy at a threshold).**  
Let (C) be a certifier language and (p\in P). (C) is _adequate at threshold (p)_ for (\lambda_\kappa) when, for every judgment shape (e), the threshold failure set

[  
F_{e,p}={\omega\in\Omega_e:\lambda_\kappa(\omega)<p}  
]

is expressible as the pullback of an upward set in the finite observation cube induced by (C).

**Definition 5.12 (Refinement order on certifier languages).**  
For certifier languages (C) and (C'), write

[  
C\preceq C'  
]

when (C') is at least as expressive as (C): for every judgment shape (e), every upward set expressible in the (C)-observation cube has an equivalent upward-set expression in the (C')-observation cube.

Equivalently, (C\preceq C') means that (C) is a coarsening of (C'), or (C') is a refinement of (C).

**Definition 5.13 (Forced refinement).**  
Let (C) be inadequate at threshold (p) for (\lambda_\kappa). A refinement (C') is _forced at (p)_ when every certifier language adequate at (p) must separate at least one pair of states that (C) identifies.

More concretely, if there exist (\omega,\omega'\in\Omega_e) such that

[  
\alpha_C(\omega)=\alpha_C(\omega')  
]

but

[  
\lambda_\kappa(\omega)\ge p  
\quad\text{and}\quad  
\lambda_\kappa(\omega')<p,  
]

then no (C)-compiler can be sharp at (p). Any adequate refinement must introduce observable structure separating (\omega) from (\omega').

**Proposition 5.14 (Separation criterion for forced refinement).**  
If (C) identifies two states on opposite sides of a threshold failure boundary, then (C) is not adequate at that threshold.

_Proof._  
Suppose (\alpha_C(\omega)=\alpha_C(\omega')), but (\lambda_\kappa(\omega)\ge p) and (\lambda_\kappa(\omega')<p). Any compiler factoring through (\alpha_C) assigns the same value to (\omega) and (\omega'). Therefore it cannot equal (\lambda_\kappa) on both states. Hence no sharp (C)-compiler exists at threshold (p). ∎

In a derived domain, such separations are forced by the domain's failure geometry. For example, if ALR requires distinguishing approximation error from model-specification error, then any language adequate at ALR must expose that distinction.

In a chosen domain, the same syntactic refinement may or may not be forced, depending on which licensing map the profile selects. A language omitting `clinical_utility_gap` may be adequate relative to a profile that does not require operating-point utility evidence. A language including it may be adequate relative to a profile that does. The refinement is not forced by the base mathematics of prediction; it is forced only after the profile has selected a stricter ALR boundary.

Thus the threshold-local chain

[  
C_{\mathrm{DIA}}  
\preceq  
C_{\mathrm{AEX}}  
\preceq  
C_{\mathrm{ALR}}  
\preceq  
C_{\mathrm{AAA}}  
]

should be read as an expressiveness chain. Each step may be derived, chosen, or mixed. A derived step is forced by threshold separation. A chosen step is imposed by profile selection.

### 5.9 Consequences

Three consequences follow.

**Consequence 1: In derived domains, convergence has mathematical authority.**  
If the domain licensing specification admits a unique map, then a sharp compiler is computing the unique admissible (\lambda_\kappa). Independent corpora and independently designed certifier languages should converge to the same threshold boundaries, provided they are adequate. Disagreement is evidence of inadequate corpus, inadequate language, or an incorrectly specified domain.

**Consequence 2: In chosen domains, convergence extracts policy.**  
If multiple licensing maps remain admissible, an induction loop run on historical judgments recovers the licensing map implicit in those judgments. That may be the institution's intended policy. It may also encode historical bias, resource constraints, or accidental practice. The loop cannot distinguish those possibilities by itself.

**Consequence 3: Profile versioning is semantic versioning.**  
In a chosen domain, changing the profile changes the selected (\lambda_\kappa). Profile versioning is therefore not bookkeeping. It is the audit trail for which ideal licensing map was in force at a given time.

The compiler's authority differs accordingly. In a derived domain, the compiler's authority comes from the mathematics determining (\lambda_\kappa). In a chosen domain, the compiler's authority comes from the governance process that selected the profile. The compiler cannot supply missing governance authority. It can only enforce the selected map consistently.

## 6. Constructive Reachability and the Over-Authorization Loop

The representation theorem is static. It says that a sharp compiler exists exactly when the threshold failure sets factor through a finite observable quotient. Section 5 explained when the target licensing map is determined by the domain and when it is selected by profile.

This section addresses a different question:

> What can an induction loop actually recover from observed over-authorization?

The answer is asymmetric. The loop is excellent at eliminating over-authorization. It is blind to under-authorization. Therefore it is a descent procedure in the permission order, not a general sharpness optimizer.

This asymmetry is not an implementation defect. It is a theorem.

### 6.1 The Support-Cube Dual

Section 4 used an obstruction cube: more (1)'s meant more obstruction, and failure sets were upward closed.

For constructive induction it is often more natural to use the dual support cube: more (1)'s mean more valid evidence. The two presentations are equivalent by Boolean complement. We use the support presentation here because the experiments are expressed as recovery of minimal sufficient certificate valuations.

Fix a judgment shape (e) and threshold permission (p). Let (J_e) be the finite set of positive support atoms available for this judgment shape. A valuation is a subset

[  
v\subseteq J_e.  
]

Write

[  
v\preceq w  
]

when (v\subseteq w). Thus (w) has at least the support present in (v).

Let

[  
B_e = 2^{J_e}  
]

be the support cube.

A threshold authorization region is a subset

[  
A^*_{e,p}\subseteq B_e  
]

where

[  
v\in A^*_{e,p}  
]

means that valuation (v) supports permission at least (p).

Because additional valid support cannot make an authorized valuation unauthorized, (A^*_{e,p}) is upward closed:

[  
v\in A^__{e,p},\ v\preceq w  
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad  
w\in A^__{e,p}.  
]

Since (B_e) is finite, every upward-closed set has a finite antichain of minimal elements.

**Definition 6.1 (Minimal authorization antichain).**  
The _minimal authorization antichain_ at threshold (p) is

[  
M^__{e,p} = \min(A^__{e,p}),  
]

the set of minimal valuations that suffice for permission (p). Then

# [  
A^*_{e,p}

# \uparrow M^*_{e,p}

{v\in B_e:\exists m\in M^*_{e,p}\text{ such that }m\preceq v}.  
]

In the obstruction presentation, this same object appears as a finite antichain of minimal failure obstructions. In the support presentation, it appears as a finite antichain of minimal sufficient evidence patterns.

### 6.2 Profiles as Antichains

A threshold profile can be represented by an antichain (M\subseteq B_e). It authorizes threshold (p) exactly on

# [  
\uparrow M

{v:\exists m\in M,\ m\preceq v}.  
]

The permissive starting profile is

[  
M_0={\varnothing},  
]

because (\varnothing\preceq v) for every valuation (v). Thus (M_0) authorizes every valuation.

**Definition 6.2 (Sound and sharp antichain profiles).**  
A profile antichain (M) is _sound_ at threshold (p) when

[  
\uparrow M \subseteq \uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

It is _sharp_ when

[  
\uparrow M = \uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

Soundness means the profile never authorizes a valuation that the oracle would refuse. Sharpness means it also authorizes every valuation the oracle would permit.

An _over-authorization witness_ for (M) is a valuation

[  
v\in \uparrow M \setminus \uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

An _under-authorization witness_ for (M) is a valuation

[  
v\in \uparrow M^*_{e,p} \setminus \uparrow M.  
]

The induction loop sees only the first kind.

### 6.3 The Over-Authorization Refinement Operator

Suppose the current profile antichain is (M_t), and the corpus contains an over-authorization witness (v^-):

[  
v^- \in \uparrow M_t  
\quad\text{but}\quad  
v^- \notin \uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

The profile authorized (v^-), but the oracle says (v^-) does not support permission (p).

The elements of (M_t) responsible for this authorization are

# [  
C_t(v^-)

{m\in M_t:m\preceq v^-}.  
]

These are the current sufficient patterns satisfied by the bad valuation.

A refinement oracle returns a minimal true sufficient pattern not satisfied by (v^-):

[  
\rho(v^-)\in M^*_{e,p}  
\quad\text{with}\quad  
\rho(v^-)\npreceq v^-.  
]

Such an element exists because (v^-\notin\uparrow M^__{e,p}), so no element of (M^__{e,p}) is contained in (v^-).

The updated profile is

# [  
M_{t+1}

\min\Bigl(  
(M_t\setminus C_t(v^-))\cup{\rho(v^-)}  
\Bigr),  
]

where (\min) removes any antichain element made redundant by a weaker element.

Intuitively, the loop removes the permissive rule that authorized the bad valuation and replaces it with a stricter sufficient-evidence pattern.

This operator is local. It specializes only the part of the profile that actually caused over-authorization.

### 6.4 Single-Antichain Recovery

The cleanest recovery theorem is the case seen in the KL threshold experiment: the true threshold has one minimal sufficient evidence pattern.

**Theorem 6.3 (Single-antichain recovery).**  
Assume the target authorization region has a singleton minimal antichain

[  
M^__{e,p}={m^_}.  
]

Start from the permissive profile

[  
M_0={\varnothing}.  
]

If the corpus contains at least one negative valuation (v^-\notin\uparrow{m^_}), then the over-authorization loop recovers (m^_) in one refinement step and the resulting profile is sharp on the full Boolean cube.

_Proof._  
Since (M_0={\varnothing}), every valuation satisfies the current profile. In particular, the negative valuation (v^-) is over-authorized:

[  
v^-\in\uparrow M_0  
\quad\text{but}\quad  
v^-\notin\uparrow{m^*}.  
]

The responsible current element is (\varnothing), because (\varnothing\preceq v^-). The refinement oracle must return an element of (M^__{e,p}). Since (M^__{e,p}) is singleton, it returns (m^*). Therefore

# [  
M_1

# \min(({\varnothing}\setminus{\varnothing})\cup{m^*})

{m^*}.  
]

Then

[  
\uparrow M_1=\uparrow{m^_}=\uparrow M^__{e,p}=A^*_{e,p}.  
]

Thus the recovered profile is sharp on the full cube. ∎

**Corollary 6.4 (Order independence in the singleton case).**  
Under the hypotheses of Theorem 6.3, every corpus ordering that exposes at least one negative valuation converges to the same antichain ({m^*}).

_Proof._  
Any negative valuation triggers the same refinement, because the target antichain has only one element. Therefore the first signal in any ordering produces (M_1={m^*}), after which the profile is sharp and no further refinement occurs. ∎

This is the formal content of the single-threshold KL experiment. For threshold (\epsilon_3=0.10), the minimal sufficient valuation is

[  
m^*=  
{\texttt{kl_bounded_eps2},\texttt{kl_bounded_eps3}}.  
]

Starting permissive, the no-certificate valuation is over-authorized. The oracle lookup returns the unique minimal valuation sufficient for AEX, and the loop converges in one step.

### 6.5 The Fundamental Asymmetry

The single-antichain theorem should not be generalized naively. The over-authorization loop cannot, by itself, guarantee full sharpness for arbitrary disjunctive authorization regions.

**Theorem 6.5 (Sound profiles are fixed points).**  
If (M) is sound, then the over-authorization loop has no signal.

That is, if

[  
\uparrow M\subseteq \uparrow M^*_{e,p},  
]

then there is no valuation (v) such that

[  
v\in\uparrow M  
\quad\text{and}\quad  
v\notin\uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

_Proof._  
This is immediate from the definition of subset inclusion. Soundness says every valuation authorized by (M) is also authorized by the target. Therefore no authorized valuation can be an over-authorization witness. ∎

**Corollary 6.6 (Under-authorization blindness).**  
If

[  
\uparrow M\subsetneq\uparrow M^*_{e,p},  
]

then (M) is sound but not sharp. The loop halts even though there exist authorized-by-oracle valuations that the profile refuses.

The missing region

[  
\uparrow M^*_{e,p}\setminus\uparrow M  
]

is under-authorized. It produces no over-authorization signal.

This is not a weakness of a particular implementation. It is information-theoretic. An over-authorization-only loop receives no event when it refuses something the oracle would have allowed.

### 6.6 Disjunctive Targets and Partial Recovery

The empirical disjunctive case is the canonical example.

Suppose

[  
M^*_{e,p}={m_1,m_2},  
]

with (m_1) and (m_2) incomparable. Then the true authorization region is

# [  
A^*_{e,p}

\uparrow m_1\cup\uparrow m_2.  
]

If the loop learns (m_1) first, the current profile is

[  
M={m_1}.  
]

Then

# [  
\uparrow M=\uparrow m_1  
\subseteq  
\uparrow m_1\cup\uparrow m_2

\uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

So the profile is sound. By Theorem 6.5, the over-authorization signal disappears. But unless

[  
\uparrow m_2\subseteq\uparrow m_1,  
]

which would contradict minimality of the antichain, there are valuations in

[  
\uparrow m_2\setminus\uparrow m_1.  
]

Those valuations are truly sufficient but refused by the learned profile.

**Proposition 6.7 (Disjunctive blindness).**  
For a target antichain with at least two incomparable elements, an over-authorization-only loop can converge to a sound strict subset of the true authorization region.

_Proof._  
Let (M^*={m_1,m_2}) with (m_1,m_2) incomparable. Suppose the first refinement returns (m_1). Then the learned profile (M={m_1}) is sound because

[  
\uparrow m_1\subseteq\uparrow m_1\cup\uparrow m_2.  
]

It is not sharp because (m_2\in\uparrow M^*) but (m_2\notin\uparrow m_1), since (m_1\npreceq m_2). Thus (m_2) itself is an under-authorization witness. By Theorem 6.5, the loop halts because the profile is sound. ∎

This exactly captures the disjunctive experiment:

[  
\mathrm{AEX}  
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad  
\mathrm{KL\ certificate}  
\quad\text{or}\quad  
\mathrm{scope\ certificate}.  
]

If the loop discovers the `scope_bounded` sufficient element first, it becomes sound and stops. The KL-only cases are under-authorized and invisible. Seeding the KL element shows that it is independently discoverable, but not discoverable from over-authorization after the scope element has already made the profile sound.

### 6.7 Sound-but-Not-Sharp Starts

The same theorem explains the strict-threshold experiment.

Suppose the true KL threshold is (\epsilon_3), but the starting profile requires a stricter certificate, such as (\epsilon_1). Let

[  
M_{\mathrm{strict}}  
]

be the stricter sufficient valuation and

[  
M^*  
]

the true sufficient valuation. If

[  
\uparrow M_{\mathrm{strict}}  
\subsetneq  
\uparrow M^*,  
]

then the starting profile is sound but not sharp.

By Theorem 6.5, the loop performs zero updates.

This is the Boolean-cube version of the earlier schema-level result: under-authorization is not mislicensing. It may be undesirable, inefficient, or overly conservative, but it does not violate the non-promotion guarantee. The over-authorization loop is designed to prevent unsupported escalation, not to maximize permission.

### 6.8 Full Antichain Recovery Requires an Additional Completeness Source

The previous results show that full recovery of (M^*) requires more than over-authorization signals. It requires an additional source of completeness.

There are several possible sources:

1. **Singleton target:** (M^*) has one element. Then Theorem 6.3 gives full recovery.
    
2. **All-minimal oracle:** the refinement oracle returns all minimal sufficient elements, not just one.
    
3. **Seed coverage:** each minimal sufficient element is seeded or independently queried.
    
4. **Positive adequacy auditing:** the loop is augmented with a signal for under-authorized positive cases.
    
5. **Domain theorem:** the domain proves that the threshold has a principal authorization region.
    

The strongest clean statement is the following.

**Theorem 6.8 (Complete antichain recovery under coverage).**  
Let (M^__{e,p}) be finite. Suppose an induction procedure inserts every element of (M^__{e,p}) and no element outside (M^*_{e,p}). Then the resulting antichain is sharp:

[  
\uparrow M = \uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

If, additionally, the insertion order is arbitrary but the final antichain is reduced by removing redundant dominated elements, the final result is order-independent.

_Proof._  
If every element of (M^*_{e,p}) is inserted and no outside element is inserted, then after antichain reduction

[  
M=M^*_{e,p}  
]

because (M^*_{e,p}) is already an antichain of minimal elements. Therefore

[  
\uparrow M=\uparrow M^*_{e,p}.  
]

The result is independent of insertion order because finite union followed by antichain minimization is commutative and idempotent. ∎

This theorem is deliberately conditional. It says what is needed for full recovery. It does not pretend that over-authorization alone supplies that condition.

### 6.9 Local Minimality and Witnesses

Each refinement step has a blocking witness: the over-authorized valuation that caused the refinement.

Suppose (M_{t+1}) is obtained from (M_t) by replacing a responsible permissive element with (\rho(v^-)). Then (v^-) is blocked by the new rule because

[  
\rho(v^-)\npreceq v^-.  
]

If that refinement is removed and the prior permissive element is restored, (v^-) is over-authorized again.

This gives a local minimality certificate.

**Definition 6.9 (Blocking witness).**  
A valuation (v^-) is a _blocking witness_ for a learned antichain element (m) relative to predecessor profile (M_{\mathrm{prev}}) when:

1. (v^-) is authorized by (M_{\mathrm{prev}});
    
2. (v^-) is not authorized by the oracle;
    
3. replacing the predecessor rule with (m) blocks (v^-).
    

In the singleton KL experiment, removing

[  
{\texttt{kl_bounded_eps2},\texttt{kl_bounded_eps3}}  
]

restores the permissive profile. The no-certificate and bad-KL cases are again over-authorized. Those cases are blocking witnesses for the learned element.

For multi-element disjunctive targets, blocking witnesses prove that a refinement was needed to eliminate over-authorization. They do not, by themselves, prove that all sufficient disjuncts have been found. Full minimality additionally requires _exclusive positive witnesses_.

**Definition 6.10 (Exclusive positive witness).**  
For (m\in M^*), a valuation (v_m) is an exclusive positive witness for (m) if

[  
m\preceq v_m  
]

and for every (m'\in M^*\setminus{m}),

[  
m'\npreceq v_m.  
]

Such a witness proves that removing (m) would create under-authorization.

Blocking witnesses certify non-promotion pressure. Exclusive positive witnesses certify sharpness pressure. The over-authorization loop observes the former automatically. It observes the latter only if positive adequacy auditing is added.

### 6.10 Witness-Rate Bound

The earlier clean-witness bound can be stated precisely as a stochastic discovery bound, not as an unconditional theorem.

Suppose there are (k) missing elements to discover, and at induction stage (i), a clean witness for the next missing element appears with probability

[  
r_i.  
]

If cases are sampled independently, the waiting time for that witness is geometric with expectation

[  
\mathbb E[T_i]=\frac{1}{r_i}.  
]

Therefore the expected number of cases to discover all (k) elements under an exploration procedure that continues until all are found is

# [  
\mathbb E[T]

\sum_{i=1}^k \frac{1}{r_i}.  
]

A common approximation decomposes

# [  
r_i

p_i(1-\bar p)^{i-1},  
]

where (p_i) is the probability that the (i)-th undiscovered obstruction or sufficient pattern is active, and ((1-\bar p)^{i-1}) approximates the probability that previously induced blockers do not mask it. Under that model,

# [  
\mathbb E[T]

\sum_{i=1}^k  
\frac{1}{p_i(1-\bar p)^{i-1}}.  
]

This is the source of the earlier corpus-size expression. It is a geometric waiting-time calculation under an independence approximation. It should not be read as a worst-case guarantee. In the worst case, an adversarial corpus can hide any missing element forever.

The practical meaning is narrower but useful: when clean witnesses occur at non-negligible rates, the discovery corpus is small; when a required witness is rare or masked by already-induced conditions, discovery becomes correspondingly difficult.

### 6.11 Causal Separation

The loop does not induce correlated features merely because they correlate with failure. It induces only boundary elements returned by the refinement oracle in response to over-authorization.

Let (s\in J_e) be a support atom that is statistically correlated with authorization but is not contained in any minimal sufficient element:

[  
s\notin \bigcup_{m\in M^*_{e,p}}m.  
]

Assume the refinement oracle returns only elements of (M^*_{e,p}).

**Proposition 6.11 (No induction of non-basis atoms).**  
Under a minimal-element refinement oracle, no atom outside the minimal authorization antichain can be introduced by the loop.

_Proof._  
The only atoms introduced by the loop are atoms contained in (\rho(v^-)) for some over-authorization witness (v^-). By assumption,

[  
\rho(v^-)\in M^*_{e,p}.  
]

Therefore every introduced atom belongs to some minimal sufficient element. If (s) belongs to no element of (M^*_{e,p}), it is never introduced. ∎

This is the formal version of causal separation. A regression over cases might rank (s) highly if it is correlated with success or failure. The induction loop does not ask which atoms are correlated. It asks which minimal boundary element eliminates an actual over-authorization.

The guarantee is asymmetric. The loop has a non-inclusion guarantee against non-basis atoms, assuming the oracle returns minimal basis elements. It does not have an inclusion guarantee for every basis element unless the completeness conditions of Section 6.8 are met.

### 6.12 What the Loop Guarantees

The constructive story is therefore:

1. **Over-authorizing start, singleton target:** the loop recovers the unique minimal antichain element and becomes sharp.
    
2. **Different corpus orders, singleton target:** the recovered element is invariant.
    
3. **Disjunctive target:** the loop may recover one sufficient element and then stop sound-but-not-sharp.
    
4. **Sound-but-not-sharp start:** the loop performs zero updates.
    
5. **Removing a learned refinement:** the original blocking witness reactivates the over-authorization signal.
    
6. **Full antichain recovery:** requires singleton structure, all-minimal oracle, seed coverage, positive adequacy auditing, or a domain theorem proving principal form.
    

Thus the over-authorization loop is not a general procedure for finding the whole of (Q_e^{\min}). It is a procedure for eliminating unsupported permission escalation.

When the target threshold is principal, eliminating over-authorization recovers the full boundary. When the target threshold is disjunctive, eliminating over-authorization may recover only a sound subset. This is exactly the correct behavior for an authorization system: it prioritizes non-promotion over maximal permission.

The representation theorem remains the existence theorem for sharp compilers. The induction loop is a constructive descent procedure whose completeness depends on additional coverage assumptions.

## 7. The Epic Example: Reconstructing Regulatory Policy

The Epic sepsis model is an unusually well-documented instance of evidence overextension. It is not a case of absent evidence. It is a case of evidence being used for a stronger permission than it supported — specifically, AUC evidence being promoted into operational rollout authority. We use it to show three things: how the compiler makes this failure precise, how compiler-processable profiles reconstruct independently published regulatory obligations, and where the framework reaches its honest boundary.

### 7.1 The Failure as a Permissions Problem

Epic's evidence package at deployment time included model specification, approximation quality (AUC 0.76–0.83), calibration data, blast-radius analysis, and freshness documentation. What it lacked:

- **Clinical utility at the deployed threshold**: AUC measures ranking across thresholds; it does not certify that the operating-point PPV or sensitivity is adequate for the intended action class (generating automatic alerts reviewed by nursing staff).
- **Distribution shift across deployment populations**: validation evidence from one institution does not automatically support deployment across dozens of hospitals with different geographies, demographics, and clinical practices.

Under the compiler framework, these two absences correspond to two open gaps: `clinical_utility_gap` and `distribution_shift_gap`. The question the framework asks is not "is Epic a good or bad model?" It is:

    What permission did Epic's evidence actually support?

Under a profile that requires only approximation quality, model specification, calibration, blast radius, and freshness:

    Γ ⊢ epic_sepsis_model : ALR until ε₁

The compiler authorizes limited rollout. This is not a compiler error. It is a profile error: the profile did not ask the questions that mattered.

Under a corrected profile that also requires clinical utility and distribution shift:

    Γ ⊬ epic_sepsis_model : ALR
    Γ ⊢ epic_sepsis_model : AEX until ε₂

The same evidence, the same model, the same data — only the policy has changed. The compiler exposes the policy boundary.

### 7.2 Reconstructing FDA and NHS Obligations

The corrected profile was not derived from the Epic case alone. It was derived by translating published regulatory guidance into compiler-processable form. This translation is the most direct demonstration of the framework's value: it turns prose obligations into mechanical enforcement objects.

**FDA Draft Guidance for AI-enabled device software functions** (Docket FDA-2024-D-4488) requires, for devices in the substantial-equivalence pathway: sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV with pre-specified acceptance criteria and confidence intervals; multi-site validation; and prospective performance data where available. Translating this into the certifier language:

- `clinical_utility_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.operating_point_validation.v1` with fields `{ppv, sensitivity, npv, specificity, confidence_intervals, acceptance_criteria_prespecified}`. A token asserting AUC alone fails the contract: AUC is not a required field.
- `distribution_shift_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.multi_site_validation.v1` with fields `{site_count, site_demographics, performance_delta_across_sites}`. A token from a single institution fails the scope check.

**NHS RCR Guidance for AI deployment in medical imaging** requires shadow-mode testing on the local population before go-live, pre-defined acceptance criteria before procurement, and an ongoing post-implementation evaluation plan. Translating:

- `shadow_mode_validation_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.shadow_mode.v1` with fields `{local_population_size, performance_on_local_cohort, pre_go_live_duration}`. A model deployed without shadow testing has this gap open.
- `post_market_monitoring_gap` → BOUNDED REQUIRED at ALR: requires a token of type `clinical.monitoring_plan.v1` with fields `{monitoring_frequency, alert_thresholds, rollback_criteria}`.

The V3 profile that includes all four of these gaps is derived entirely from published regulatory text. It was not fitted to the Epic case. The same profile, applied to the CHARTwatch study — where clinical utility validation was conducted on the local population before deployment — compiles to ALR because the required tokens can be constructed from the published evidence.

The profile translation is constructive: given regulatory prose, a domain expert can produce a compiler-processable profile. The compiler then enforces that profile mechanically. What was previously "we require adequate clinical validation" — a phrase that can be interpreted to approve or refuse the same case depending on who reads it — becomes a structured object with concrete field requirements, semantic checks, and scope rules.

### 7.3 The Boundary: Where the Compiler Leans on Priors

The reconstruction also shows where the compiler is not self-sufficient. Two kinds of boundary cases arise.

**Threshold choices are outside the compiler.** The compiler can enforce "PPV ≥ 0.20 at the deployed threshold." It cannot derive 0.20 as the correct floor. A domain expert can set 0.15 or 0.25, write a well-formed profile, and the compiler will enforce whichever choice was made. The compiler is Lipschitz in the profile (Lemma M from the structural paper): a profile that differs from the intended profile by k entries cannot produce an output more than k permission steps wrong. But the compiler cannot identify which profile is correct.

This is not a deficiency. A threshold choice requires clinical judgment about the downstream consequences of false positives and false negatives in a specific care context. No mechanical system should make that choice. The compiler's value is that once the choice is made and recorded, it is enforced consistently rather than case-by-case.

**The compiler is anchored to its certifier language.** The over-authorization signal in the Epic case revealed `clinical_utility_gap` and `distribution_shift_gap` as necessary. But it could only reveal gaps whose types were expressible in the certifier language. Two gap types — `individual_population_gap` (population-level calibration does not certify individual-level prediction) and `feedback_coupling_gap` (model deployment changes future training distributions through its own outputs) — were not in the initial taxonomy. They were induced retrospectively from other documented harmful deployments.

This is the honest limit of constructive reachability. Theorem 6.2 says the loop reaches Q_e^min given a corpus containing clean witnesses. But Q_e^min is relative to the current certifier language C. If C does not contain a schema for a gap type, that type cannot be induced, and the converged profile may be sharp relative to C while remaining unsound relative to the true ideal licensing map. The certifier language is not automatically complete.

In the Epic case, a profile containing all four V3 gaps — clinical utility, distribution shift, shadow mode, and monitoring plan — would have blocked the harmful deployment. That profile is derivable from FDA and NHS guidance. The gap between the language of published regulatory text and the language of a first-pass certifier is where the framework depends on domain expertise to expand the taxonomy.

**The causal separation theorem sets a bound on spurious induction but not on omission.** Proposition 6.4 guarantees that the loop never induces a gap that is merely correlated with failure but not structurally required. It does not guarantee that every structurally required gap is in the certifier language. The asymmetry is: the compiler has a rigorous non-inclusion guarantee (no spurious gaps) but only a contingent inclusion guarantee (required gaps, if they can be expressed).

This boundary — rigorous enforcement of what is stated, honest silence about what is not stated — is the right posture for a formal system acting in a domain where human judgment is not eliminable. The Epic case demonstrates both sides: the compiler can recover the regulatory intent once the gap types are named, and it cannot recover intent it has no language for.

## 8. Implementation

The judgment form is

    Γ ⊢ z : p until ε

where Γ is the proof context, z is the candidate artifact or action, p is the emitted permission, and ε is the expiry condition.

The proof context Γ contains: membership, claim, candidate, context, scope, induced gaps, gap taxonomy version, gap profile version, proof tokens, proof token provenance, detail-contract registry version, expiry, allowed use, disallowed use, derivation, authority, runtime context, and audit.

The compiler:

1. Checks membership; emits OOC if out of class.
2. Induces the claim and gap set under fixed taxonomy version.
3. Checks expiry; emits EXP if fired.
4. For each token: checks registry validity, detail-contract conformance, liveness, and scope. Advances gap statuses only through valid witnesses.
5. Records structural failures (provenance mismatch, allowed-use conflict, scope empty, derivation invalid, failed negative controls, runtime context failure).
6. Applies authority ceilings, escalation requirements, rollback controls.
7. Searches positive permissions strongest-to-weakest; returns first satisfiable.
8. Meets positive result with structural failures and control outcomes.
9. Emits judgment and records blocking reasons for every denied stronger permission.

The core enforcement discipline: no approximation may be used as stronger permission than its evidence, scope, provenance, expiry, authority, and policy profile jointly support.

**Structural guarantees.** The following follow from the total order on P and standard lattice observations and are recorded without detailed proof: invalid tokens do not close gaps; no provenance means no proof; expired evidence cannot remain live; runtime can only downgrade; composition cannot upgrade weak evidence; authority ceilings cannot be exceeded; profile, taxonomy, and contract version changes require a new compile; absent required gaps fail closed; the emitted permission is no stronger than the profile, evidence, provenance, authority, scope, and expiry jointly support. These properties are consequences of the finite total order and the monotonicity established in the representation theorem. They are not independent contributions.

**Non-promotion under composition.** For n ≥ 1 envelopes, composed permission is the meet of component permissions. A stale or downgraded component cannot be hidden by composition with a fresh one. Decomposition cannot upgrade a child above its parent. Runtime revalidation can only downgrade. These are all consequences of meet in a total order.

**Benchmarks.** The implementation was validated against three benchmark suites:

*Structural tests* (EC-003 and EC-004): 22,423 combined tests covering the algebra, composition, decomposition, runtime, anti-laundering, token reuse, provenance identity, rollback, profile well-formedness, adapter determinism, and adversarial domain suites. Zero falsifications.

*Medical prospective benchmark* (MED-001): real models against PhysioNet Challenge 2019 data. 11/11 oracle cases and 9/9 adversarial cases, zero violations. The V1 → V2 → V3 profile progression was produced by this benchmark: V1 authorizes the Epic failure pattern; V2 adds clinical_utility_gap and distribution_shift_gap; V3 adds shadow_mode_validation_gap and post_market_monitoring_gap. Each profile transition was forced by matching the profile against a failure case or external regulatory standard.

*Retrospective audit* (CASE-LIB-001): 16 real-world AI deployments with documented harmful outcomes across medical, criminal justice, employment, autonomous systems, and government benefits. Three pre-registered profile tiers. Tier 1 — a single four-gap cross-domain profile requiring approximation quality, model specification, blast radius, and authority — blocked all 16 cases. The profile was derived independently from the observation that these four gap types recur across domains; it was not fitted to the cases.

**Library.** The compiler, profile registry, gap taxonomy, detail-contract registry, and benchmark suite are available as an open-source library. The library is immediately deployable: a domain adopter supplies a profile and a certifier for their gap types; the compiler handles the rest.

## 9. Tameness Regimes

The representation theorem requires that threshold failure sets admit finite C-obstruction bases. When does this hold? The abstract answer is: when the domain has some tameness principle that guarantees finite observable descriptions of upward-closed sets under the worsening preorder.

Two representative routes:

**Well-quasi-ordered discrete domains.** A preorder (X, ⪯) is a well-quasi-order (WQO) when every infinite sequence contains an increasing pair. In a WQO, every upward-closed set has finitely many minimal elements (otherwise the minimal elements form an infinite antichain). If the worsening preorder on Ωκ is a WQO, every F_p has a finite minimal bad basis, and the certifier language need only contain principal-upset atoms {ω : m ⪯κ ω} for certifiable minimal bad states m. Dickson's lemma (finite products of WQOs are WQO), Higman's theorem (finite-alphabet words under subsequence are WQO), and Kruskal's tree theorem give the classical examples.

**Semialgebraic approximation domains.** If hidden ideal variables live in R^m, visible state variables in R^n, and the compatibility relation and permission failure conditions are semialgebraic, then the visible failure set F_p^vis = {v : ∃s. Compat(s,v) ∧ Fail_p(s,v)} is the projection of a semialgebraic set. By Tarski-Seidenberg, projections of semialgebraic sets are semialgebraic. A semialgebraic set has a finite formula using polynomial equalities and inequalities, which serves as the obstruction basis. Threshold conditions in deployment practice — PPV ≥ t, calibration error ≤ δ, freshness age ≤ T — are semialgebraic by construction, making this route natural for real-valued evidence systems.

The effective smallness question — whether the basis is computable, not merely existent — is answered for deployment-class domains by the witness-rate bound of Theorem 6.2: for k gap types with open probabilities p_i, the expected discovery corpus is polynomial in k and linear in 1/p_i. For k = 6 and p_i = 0.4, approximately 200–400 cases suffice at the 99th percentile. Doubly exponential quantifier elimination is not a practical obstruction because the basis is induced empirically, not computed symbolically.

Other tameness regimes — Noetherian failure loci with finitely generated ideals, o-minimal structures with finite cell decompositions, tropical/polyhedral settings with finite polyhedral shadows — follow the same meta-principle:

    monotone threshold failure
    + finite/tame observable presentation
    ⟹ finite obstruction basis
    ⟹ admissibility compiler.

## 10. Open Problems

The representation theorem is static: it identifies when the projection object Q_e^min exists. Three subsequent theorems remain open.

**Composition.** For issued envelopes, conservative composition should satisfy a lax monoidal law in the obstruction order: α(E₁ ⊗ E₂) ⊒ α(E₁) ∨ α(E₂), meaning composition cannot hide an obstruction present in a component. Exact composition — equality — is stronger and generally false when evidence components are complementary. The gap between lax and strong monoidal composition is the operational signature of evidence laundering: a fresh component can obscure a stale one unless composition is forced through meet rules. A composition theorem would characterize precisely when lax-but-not-strong is the right regime.

**Expiry dynamics.** Static expiry is a monotone obstruction atom and fits the representation theorem without change. A dynamic theorem would define runtime operators R_t : Envelope → Envelope satisfying R_0 = id, R_{s+t} = R_s ∘ R_t, R_t(E).permission ≤ E.permission — a killed semigroup in stochastic settings. This requires a model of time evolution for evidence, context, authority, and expiry rules, which is not implied by the static representation.

**Geometric families.** The WQO and semialgebraic corollaries are two instances of a broader pattern. The Noetherian, o-minimal, and tropical routes are expected to yield analogous results by their respective finite-structure theorems. A unified geometric framework for admissible compilability across tameness regimes would clarify when the meta-principle applies and what effective bounds it produces.

## 11. Related Work

The closest ancestors of the admissibility compiler are the access-control and authorization calculi of the 1990s and 2000s. Abadi, Burrows, Lampson, and Plotkin gave an access-control calculus for distributed systems; Necula introduced proof-carrying code; Appel and Felten introduced proof-carrying authentication; Myers gave JFlow for information-flow control; Schneider characterized enforceable security policies. These systems ask whether an exact artifact satisfies an exact property. The contribution here is the extension to approximate artifacts whose authority depends on evidence, scope, provenance, expiry, and policy profile — objects none of the exact frameworks can directly handle.

Model cards, datasheets, AI governance frameworks, clinical validation standards, and legal AI audits address adjacent documentation problems. They specify or describe evidence. But documentation alone does not prevent downstream promotion. The novelty here is the compiled object and admissibility discipline: permission-valued judgments, explicit proof contexts, ordered permission levels, gap profiles, exact evidence provenance, live scoped evidence, expiry, immutable envelopes, runtime non-upgrade, and compiler-processable policy. This is not "formal methods for AI" generally. It is an authorization discipline for approximate evidence whose novelty lies in the representation theorem characterizing when such a discipline can be sharp.

The WQO tameness corollary has antecedents in the theory of well-structured transition systems (Abdulla et al., Finkel and Schnoebelen), where upward-closed sets and minimal bad sequences provide finite representations for infinite-state reachability problems. The connection is structural: both exploit upward closure under a well-quasi-order to produce finite descriptions. The action-state space here is not a transition system but the parallel is genuine.

## 12. Conclusion

An approximate consequential system is admissibly compilable — admits a bounded, sharp, monotone compiler — exactly when its ideal licensing map factors through a finite monotone certifier-observable quotient. This is the canonical object Q_e^min, and it is what the compiler evaluates.

In mathematically grounded domains, λκ is uniquely determined by the approximation structure: the certifier language is the language of that map's failure geometry, the induction loop discovers a unique taxonomy, and the compiler's authority is grounded in mathematics. In policy-grounded domains, λκ is constituted by the profile: multiple action-monotone licensing maps are consistent with the domain's constraints, the profile commits to one of them, and the framework's value is not that it selects the correct commitment but that it makes the commitment explicit, machine-checkable, and auditable.

The Epic example shows both faces. The failure was not hidden: the evidence was publicly available, the deployment widely documented, the harm measurable. What was missing was a mechanical boundary between evidence and permission. Under the framework, that boundary is expressed as a profile, enforced by a compiler, and — once reconstructed from FDA and NHS guidance — would have blocked the deployment.

The compiler does not make weak evidence strong. It gives weak evidence a weak license, strong evidence a stronger license, and unsupported escalation an auditable refusal.
